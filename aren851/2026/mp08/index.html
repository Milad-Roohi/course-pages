1. Pros of dual floor/roof span-rated sheathing

What you asked: “Is dual floor/roof span-rated sheathing just more economical, or are there other pros?”

Cost is the primary driver for selecting sheathing that can be used for both roof and floor. Dual-rated panels also make it easier for the contractor to stock a single material and — perhaps — benefit from bulk pricing.

Not every span rating is readily available in every thickness. Reference Table C9.2.3 in the NDS for availability notes tied to span ratings and thicknesses.

2. Meaning of span-rating names (e.g. 24/0)

What you asked: “What do the span-rating names mean (for example, what does 24/0 imply)?”

In a two-digit sheathing span rating:

  • First digit = maximum centerline spacing (inches) between supporting members for a roof application.
  • Second digit = maximum centerline spacing (inches) for a floor application.

A single-digit label such as 16oc means the maximum centerline spacing for a floor application.

In 24/0, the “0” means the sheathing cannot be used for a floor application.

Sheathing looks very similar across span ratings; the main visible differences are number of plies (layers) and thickness.

4. Loads behind APA span ratings (40/24, 48/24)

What you asked: “Do grading agencies spell out the loads behind ratings like 40/24 or 48/24?”

APA span ratings are not based on a single uniform design load (psf). They are developed for acceptable performance under a combination of uniform loads, concentrated loads, impact loads, deflection limits, and floor vibration / serviceability criteria.

For typical code-conforming construction, APA states that a panel installed at its rated span will support at least:

  • Roof sheathing: 30 psf live + 10 psf dead
  • Floor sheathing: 40 psf live + 10 psf dead

So the rating is a performance package at the stated span, not “designed for one PSF number.”

FYI — HW3 Problem 4 chords

Not a muddiest theme — a short clarification from Prof. Feldman on HW3 Problem 4.

The problem says the chords use two 2× members. Because of the building dimensions, a splice is required. At the splice, only one member resists tension, so chord size is based on the capacity of a single member. Connection details are not involved in that size check.
