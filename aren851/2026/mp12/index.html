1. Engineered vs non-engineered timber — when is a seal required?

What you asked: Where is the cutoff between engineered and non-engineered timber projects? Span tables exist for framing and sheathing; does every house need a PE stamp?

Typical homes are exempt from a seal under Nebraska’s engineering and architecture practice acts. Detached one- to four-family dwellings with not more than 5,000 sq ft of above-grade finished space generally do not require a professional seal.

A seal is needed when any of the following apply:

  • The dwelling exceeds those size or unit limits (more than 5,000 sq ft above-grade finished, or more than four units).
  • The work includes engineered components that are not covered by a simple span-table selection — for example structural design beyond the tables, foundation design, or retaining walls.
  • Local rules are stricter than the state exemption (always check the AHJ).

Remodels or additions to one- to four-family dwellings up to three stories can stay exempt if they do not adversely affect structural, MEP, or egress systems.

Span tables let you select many common joists, rafters, and sheathing layouts without a project-specific calculation. That is still “design,” but it is prescriptive. Crossing into engineered design (or exceeding the dwelling exemptions) is what triggers a sealed set.

2. Table 11.3.3 and the wet-service factor \\(C_m\\)

What you asked: How do the inequalities in Table 11.3.3 work, and how was \\(C_m\\) chosen for the dowel-type fastener problem?

NDS Table 11.3.3 gives wet-service factors \\(C_m\\) for connections. Which row applies depends on (a) the fastener type and (b) the moisture condition of the wood in service and, in some cases, at fabrication.

For the in-class problem:

  • The statement said moisture content in service exceeds 19% (wet service).
  • The fasteners were 16d nails, which are dowel-type fasteners.
  • Therefore, for that wet-service case, \\(C_m = 0.7\\).
$$Z' = Z \\times \\cdots \\times C_m, \\qquad C_m = 0.7 \\;\\text{(16d nails, MC in service }> 19\\%)$$

When the wood is wet in service, moisture content at fabrication does not change the wet-service \\(C_m\\) for that row. If instead the wood stays dry in service (MC \\(<\\) 19%), then \\(C_m\\) depends on the moisture condition at fabrication — that is the other set of inequalities in the table.

Reading tip. First decide wet vs dry in service. Only if the member stays dry do you walk the fabrication-MC branch of Table 11.3.3.

  • NDS Chapter 11 / 12 connection provisions: Table 11.3.3 (wet-service factor \\(C_m\\) for fasteners).
