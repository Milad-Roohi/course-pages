Common failure modes for concrete masonry unit walls

Prof. Feldman obtained and fact-checked the following summary (prepared with Copilot).

1. Out-of-plane bending failure

Occurs when wind, seismic, soil, or blast loads push the wall perpendicular to its face.

Indicators:

  • Horizontal cracking near mid-height.
  • Wall bulging or bowing.
  • Collapse of large wall panels.

Common causes:

  • Insufficient vertical reinforcement.
  • Excessive wall height-to-thickness ratio.
  • Inadequate anchorage at roof or floor diaphragms.

2. In-plane shear failure

Occurs when lateral loads act within the plane of the wall.

Indicators:

  • Diagonal stair-step cracks through mortar joints.
  • X-shaped cracking patterns.
  • Sliding along bed joints.

Common causes:

  • Earthquake loads.
  • Wind shear forces.
  • Insufficient horizontal reinforcement.

3. Compression crushing

Occurs when axial loads exceed masonry compressive capacity.

Indicators:

  • Crushed mortar joints.
  • Spalled face shells.
  • Vertical splitting near heavily loaded areas.

Common causes:

  • Overloaded bearing walls.
  • Concentrated beam or truss reactions.
  • Poor grout consolidation.

4. Vertical flexural cracking

Occurs from differential movement, thermal expansion, or foundation settlement.

Indicators:

  • Vertical cracks through units and mortar joints.
  • Cracks near corners or openings.

Common causes:

  • Foundation settlement.
  • Inadequate control joints.
  • Shrinkage and temperature effects.

5. Stair-step cracking

One of the most frequently observed CMU distress patterns. (Often a serviceability issue rather than an immediate structural failure.)

Indicators:

  • Cracks follow mortar joints in a stair-step pattern.

Common causes:

  • Differential settlement.
  • Thermal movement.
  • Minor shear stresses.

6. Sliding failure

Entire portions of wall slide along a mortar bed joint.

Indicators:

  • Horizontal crack extending across the wall.
  • Lateral offset of masonry courses.

Common causes:

  • Seismic loading.
  • Poor mortar bond.
  • Insufficient shear reinforcement.

7. Anchorage failure

The wall itself remains intact, but connections fail. (This is a particularly common issue in older masonry buildings during high-wind events.)

Indicators:

  • Separation from roof diaphragm.
  • Pullout of anchors.
  • Wall collapse at supports.

Common causes:

  • Undersized ledger or tie connections.
  • Corroded anchors.
  • Poor detailing.

8. Reinforcement corrosion

Water infiltration causes steel reinforcement to rust. (Rust expands several times the volume of the original steel, generating tensile stresses that masonry cannot resist.)

Indicators:

  • Longitudinal cracking.
  • Masonry spalling.
  • Rust staining.

9. Foundation settlement failure

Movement of the supporting foundation creates stresses in the wall.

Indicators:

  • Diagonal cracks emanating from corners of openings.
  • Stair-step cracks.
  • Doors and windows becoming misaligned.

10. Bearing failure at openings

Common above doors, windows, and garage openings.

Indicators:

  • Cracking at lintel ends.
  • Crushed masonry beneath concentrated loads.
  • Sagging lintels.

Common causes:

  • Undersized steel lintels.
  • Corrosion of embedded steel.
  • Excessive roof or floor reactions.
