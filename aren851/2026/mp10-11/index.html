1. Diaphragm factor \(C_{di}\)

What you asked: What is the diaphragm factor, and how does it change capacity?

Through the 2018 NDS, \(C_{di} = 1.1\) increased the lateral (shear) design value of nails and spikes used in diaphragm construction. It reflects how wood structural panel diaphragms behave as a system: diaphragm edge nailing develops load sharing and deformation that a single isolated fastener test does not show, so the assembly reliably carries a bit more than individual nail values predict.

$$Z' = Z \times \cdots \times C_{di}, \qquad C_{di} = 1.1 \;\text{(NDS 2018 and earlier)}$$

The factor was removed from the 2024 NDS. It was not dropped for being unconservative or wrong. Diaphragm-specific adjustments belong in the SDPWS (Special Design Provisions for Wind and Seismic), not the general fastener provisions of the NDS.

2. Toenail inspection

What you asked: How do special inspectors make sure toenails are installed properly? Are they common enough not to worry about?

Special inspectors do not check every toenail. They verify that the framing crew follows the approved plans and that representative connections are installed per the construction documents and code. In Prof. Feldman’s words, it is usually obvious when a framing crew doesn’t know what it is doing.

3. The reduction term \(R_d\)

What you asked: The \(R_d\) reduction term was hard to follow.

The yield-limit equations come from the European Yield Model (EYM). They give the load at which a connection reaches a failure mechanism: wood bearing, fastener yielding, or a combination. Those theoretical loads are relatively high. Rather than change the mechanics, the NDS divides each yield load by \(R_d\) to get the reference lateral design value \(Z\), consistent with the reliability and historical performance basis of wood connection design.

$$Z = \frac{\text{EYM yield load for the mode}}{R_d}$$

Some modes are predicted more accurately than others, so NDS Table 12.3.1B assigns a different \(R_d\) to each yield mode. Use the \(R_d\) for the mode you are evaluating, not one value for all.

Revised Example 7o1. Prof. Feldman corrected the \(R_d\) calculation in Example 7o1 (bolt, single shear, loaded at an angle). Use the revised file posted in Module 7.

4. What the yield modes mean

What you asked: What do the different modes mean, starting on page 5 of the example?

A connection has many components with different strengths and sizes: fastener diameter, penetration, member thickness, member stiffness, fastener yield strength. Each mode is one of those components reaching its limit state.

ModeWhat governs
\(\mathrm{I_m}\)Crushing (bearing) of the main member under the fastener over its thickness
\(\mathrm{I_s}\)Crushing (bearing) of the side member under the fastener over its thickness
II, III, IVThe fastener is stiff relative to the members, so one or more plastic hinges form in the fastener, producing uneven bearing on the wood

Compute every applicable mode. The lowest value controls the connection capacity.
