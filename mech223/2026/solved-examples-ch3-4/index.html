Example 1 · Minimum Distance for a Required Moment

Source: Individual Homework 5, Q1 (Canvas 20760; key HW5_S). Ch. 3, moment of a force about a point.

Problem. A force with a moment of $840\ \text{N·m}$ about D is required to straighten the fence post CD. The capacity of winch puller AB is $2100\ \text{N}$. Determine the minimum distance $d$ that creates the specified moment about D.

(Figure described in words: D is at the origin on the ground; the post top C is at $(0.25,\,0.90)\ \text{m}$; the cable runs from C down to ground point E at $x=-d$.)

Strategy. The moment about D is the tension times the perpendicular distance $p$ from D to the cable line. Express $p$ in terms of $d$ with a cross product, set $M_D=840\ \text{N·m}$ at the maximum tension, and solve. A larger $d$ gives a larger lever arm, so the equality gives the minimum $d$.

Key equations.

$$p=\frac{0.90\,d}{\sqrt{(d+0.25)^2+0.90^2}},\qquad M_D=T\,p=2100\,\frac{0.90\,d}{\sqrt{(d+0.25)^2+0.90^2}}$$ $$0.16\left[(d+0.25)^2+0.90^2\right]=0.81\,d^2\;\Longrightarrow\;0.65\,d^2-0.08\,d-0.1396=0$$
$$\boxed{d=0.529\ \text{m}}$$

Check. With $d=0.529$: $\sqrt{0.779^2+0.81}\approx1.19$, so $p\approx0.40\ \text{m}$ and $M_D\approx 2100(0.40)=840\ \text{N·m}$.

Match to key Matches HW5_S: $d=0.529\ \text{m}$ (same quadratic, positive root).

Example 2 · Moments of a Crate's Weight About a Bottom Point

Source: Individual Homework 5, Q2 (Canvas 20760; key HW5_S). Ch. 3, moment of a force and smallest force for a given moment.

Problem. A crate of mass $60\ \text{kg}$ is held in position. It is $1.4\ \text{m}$ wide and $0.6\ \text{m}$ tall. Point E is on the bottom edge, $1.0\ \text{m}$ from the right end D. Determine (a) the moment of the weight $W$ ($g=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$) about E; (b) the smallest force at A that creates a moment of equal magnitude and opposite sense about E; (c) the magnitude, sense, and point of application on the bottom of the crate of the smallest vertical force that does the same.

(Figure described in words: A is the top-left corner, B the top-right, D the bottom-right; the center of gravity is at mid-width.)

Strategy. $W$ acts at mid-width, so its moment about E is $W$ times the horizontal distance to E. For (b), a force of fixed moment is smallest when its lever arm is largest, so push perpendicular to line EA. For (c), a vertical force's lever arm is its horizontal distance from E, so apply it as far from E as possible, at D.

Key equations.

$$W=60(9.81)=588.6\ \text{N},\qquad d_e=0.7-0.4=0.3\ \text{m},\qquad M_E=W\,d_e$$ $$\vec r_{EA}=(-0.4,\,0.6)\ \text{m},\quad |\vec r_{EA}|=\sqrt{0.52}=0.7211\ \text{m},\qquad F_{\min}=\frac{M_E}{|\vec r_{EA}|},\qquad F_v=\frac{M_E}{1.0}$$
$$\text{(a)}\ \boxed{M_E=176.58\ \text{N·m, clockwise}}$$ $$\text{(b)}\ \boxed{F_{\min}=244.9\ \text{N at A, perpendicular to EA, down and to the left}}$$ $$\text{(c)}\ \boxed{F_v=176.58\ \text{N upward, applied at D}}$$

Check. $244.9(0.7211)\approx176.6$ and $176.58(1.0)=176.58$, both CCW about E. (b) exceeds (c) because its lever arm is shorter.

Match to key Matches HW5_S for all three parts. Flag: the key's text rounds $|\vec r_{EA}|$ to $0.72\ \text{m}$; the printed $244.9\ \text{N}$ uses the unrounded $0.7211\ \text{m}$ (dividing by exactly $0.72$ gives $245.3\ \text{N}$).

Example 3 · Collars on Perpendicular Rods Joined by a Wire

Source: Individual Homework 5, Q3 (Canvas 20760; key HW5_S). Ch. 3–4, 3D equilibrium of a particle on a smooth guide.

Problem. Collars A and B are connected by a $25\ \text{in}$ wire and slide freely on frictionless rods. A $90\ \text{lb}$ force $Q$ is applied to collar B. Determine (a) the tension in the wire when $x=12\ \text{in}$, and (b) the force $P$ needed for equilibrium. The vertical distance between the rods is $20\ \text{in}$.

(Figure described in words: B slides on a vertical rod along $z$ and carries $Q$ upward; A slides on a horizontal rod parallel to $x$ and carries $P$ along that rod.)

Strategy. Use the wire-length constraint to find the height $z$ of B. Each smooth rod can only push perpendicular to itself, so along each rod only the wire component and the applied force matter. Balance B along its rod for $T$, then A along its rod for $P$.

Key equations. With $A=(x,20,0)$ and $B=(0,0,z)$:

$$L^2=x^2+20^2+z^2\;\Rightarrow\;z=\sqrt{25^2-12^2-20^2}=9\ \text{in}$$ $$\text{B: } Q-T\frac{z}{L}=0\;\Rightarrow\;T=\frac{QL}{z}=\frac{90(25)}{9},\qquad \text{A: } P-T\frac{x}{L}=0\;\Rightarrow\;P=250\cdot\frac{12}{25}$$
$$\boxed{T=250\ \text{lb}}\qquad\boxed{P=120\ \text{lb}}$$

Check. Direction ratios $(12,20,9)/25$ satisfy $12^2+20^2+9^2=625=25^2$. $T_z=250(9/25)=90\ \text{lb}=Q$ and $T_x=250(12/25)=120\ \text{lb}=P$.

Match to key Matches HW5_S: $T=250\ \text{lb}$, $P=120\ \text{lb}$.

Example 4 · Bent Plate: Equivalent Force–Couple System

Source: Group Homework 5, Q1 (Canvas 20760; key GHW5_S). Ch. 3, equivalent force–couple systems in 3D.

Problem. A bent plate has a vertical part in the y-z plane (height $120\ \text{mm}$, width $60\ \text{mm}$) and a horizontal part in the x-y plane extending a distance $L$ along $+x$ from the junction C. Corner B is at the top of the vertical part; corner E is at the free end of the horizontal part. A force $\vec F_1=(50,\,80,\,F_z)\ \text{N}$ and a couple $\vec M_1=(M_x,\,20,\,-10)\ \text{N·m}$ act at E. The system is replaced by an equivalent force–couple system $(\vec F_2,\vec M_2)$ at B, with $(M_2)_z=0$, $(M_2)_y=1.5\ \text{N·m}$, $(M_2)_x=-8\ \text{N·m}$. Determine (a) $L$, (b) $F_z$ and $M_x$, (c) $\vec F_2$ and $\vec M_2$.

Strategy. Moving the system from E to B leaves the force unchanged and adds the moment of $\vec F_1$ about B. The three given components of $\vec M_2$ give three scalar equations for $L$, $F_z$, $M_x$, solved in the order $z$, $y$, $x$.

Key equations. Following the key's coordinates, $B=(0,0.12,0)$ and $E=(L,0,0)$ m, so $\vec r_{BE}=(L,-0.12,0)$ m.

$$\vec M_2=\vec M_1+\vec r_{BE}\times\vec F_1=\bigl(M_x-0.12F_z,\;20-LF_z,\;-10+80L+6\bigr)\ \text{N·m}$$ $$(M_2)_z=0:\ 80L-4=0\Rightarrow L=0.05\ \text{m};\qquad (M_2)_y=1.5:\ F_z=\frac{18.5}{0.05}=370\ \text{N};\qquad (M_2)_x=-8:\ M_x=-8+0.12(370)$$
$$\boxed{L=50\ \text{mm}}\qquad\boxed{F_z=370\ \text{N}}\qquad\boxed{M_x=36.4\ \text{N·m}}$$ $$\boxed{\vec F_2=(50,\,80,\,370)\ \text{N}}\qquad\boxed{\vec M_2=(-8,\,1.5,\,0)\ \text{N·m}}$$

Check. $-10+80(0.05)+6=0$; $20-0.05(370)=1.5$; $36.4-0.12(370)=-8$.

Match to key Matches GHW5_S for every result. Flag (geometry): the problem text and figure put the $120\ \text{mm}$ height along $z$, but GHW5_S places B at $(0,0.12,0)$. The numbers above follow the key; with B at $(0,0,0.12)$ the cross product would change. Clarify before reuse.

Example 5 · Two Couples Replaced by One Equivalent Couple

Source: Group Homework 5, Q2 (Canvas 20760; key GHW5_S). Ch. 3, couple moments in 3D.

Problem. Couple 1: two $80\ \text{N}$ forces along $z$, $+z$ at $A(0.1,\,0.2,\,0.3)$ m and $-z$ at $B(0.1,\,-0.2,\,0.3)$ m. Couple 2: two $60\ \text{N}$ forces along $x$, $+x$ at $C(-0.2,\,0.1,\,0)$ m and $-x$ at $D(0.2,\,0.1,\,0.2)$ m. Replace them with a single equivalent couple; find its magnitude and the unit vector along its axis. (Given by coordinates only; no figure.)

Strategy. Compute each couple as $\vec M=\vec r\times\vec F$, with $\vec r$ running from the $-\vec F$ point to the $+\vec F$ point. Couple moments are free vectors, so add them and normalize.

Key equations.

$$\vec M_1=(0,\,0.4,\,0)\times(0,0,80)=(32,0,0),\qquad \vec M_2=(-0.4,\,0,\,-0.2)\times(60,0,0)=(0,-12,0)\ \text{N·m}$$ $$\vec M=(32,-12,0)\ \text{N·m},\qquad |\vec M|=\sqrt{32^2+12^2},\qquad \hat u=\frac{\vec M}{|\vec M|}$$
$$\boxed{\vec M=(32,\,-12,\,0)\ \text{N·m},\quad |\vec M|=34.18\ \text{N·m},\quad \hat u=(0.936,\,-0.351,\,0)}$$

Check. $0.936^2+0.351^2\approx1$. Couple 1 alone is $80(0.4)=32$; couple 2 alone is $60(0.2)=12$ (only the $0.2\ \text{m}$ z-offset is perpendicular to the force line).

Match to key Agrees with GHW5_S numerically. Flag (typo in key): the key's text once writes “$+F_1=90\hat k$” (90 vs 80). Its calculation correctly uses $80\ \text{N}$, and $(32,0,0)$ requires 80.

Example 6 · Moment of a Slanted Force About the Base of a Boom

Source: Individual Homework 6, Q1 (Canvas 20760; key Solution-HW6, Canvas 8480, Fall 2025). Ch. 3, moment by components (Varignon).

Problem. Calculate the magnitude of the moment about point O due to the applied force. (Figure described in words: the boom base is O; A is $4\ \text{m}$ above O and $2\ \text{m}$ to the right; a $600\ \text{N}$ force acts at A, $40^\circ$ below the horizontal, down and to the right.)

Strategy. Resolve the force into horizontal and vertical components at A and take each one's moment about O using its perpendicular distance. Both tend to rotate the boom clockwise, so they add.

Key equations.

$$F_x=600\cos40^\circ\approx459.6\ \text{N},\qquad F_y=600\sin40^\circ\approx385.7\ \text{N}$$ $$M_O=F_x(4)+F_y(2)=1838.4+771.4$$
$$\boxed{M_O\approx2610\ \text{N·m (clockwise)}}$$

Check. Upper bound $600\sqrt{4^2+2^2}\approx2683\ \text{N·m}$; the result is just below it because the force is nearly perpendicular to OA.

Match to key Matches Solution-HW6: $2610\ \text{N·m}$. Note: the key rounds components to $460\ \text{N}$ and $386\ \text{N}$ (giving 2612) and reports 2610; unrounded values give $2609.8$.

Example 7 · Reactions on a Beam Welded to an L-Shaped Member

Source: Individual Homework 6, Q2 (Canvas 20760; key Solution-HW6, Canvas 8480, Fall 2025). Ch. 4, 2D rigid-body equilibrium.

Problem. Beam AB is welded at C to member CDE and carries the loading shown. Determine the support reactions at A and B. (Figure described in words: A is a pin, B is a roller; a $5\ \text{kN}$ force acts downward at E; a $9\ \text{kN·m}$ clockwise couple acts on CDE; the dimension line reads $3\ \text{m}$ from A to the line of the $5\ \text{kN}$ force and $5\ \text{m}$ from there to B.)

Strategy. Take the free body of the whole welded assembly. The pin gives $A_x,A_y$; the roller gives only $B_y$. Take moments about B to find $A_y$ directly, then use the force equations. The couple is a free vector, so its location does not matter.

Key equations. (Clockwise positive about B.)

$$\sum M_B=0:\ 8A_y-5(5)+9=0\Rightarrow A_y=2\ \text{kN}$$ $$\sum F_x=0:\ A_x=0,\qquad \sum F_y=0:\ B_y-5+2=0\Rightarrow B_y=3\ \text{kN}$$
$$\boxed{A_x=0}\qquad\boxed{A_y=2\ \text{kN}\ \uparrow}\qquad\boxed{B_y=3\ \text{kN}\ \uparrow}$$

Check. Moments about A: $8(3)-5(3)-9=0$; vertical forces: $2+3=5\ \text{kN}$.

Match to key Matches Solution-HW6. Flag (dimensions): the figure's dimension line breaks under the $5\ \text{kN}$ force at E, not at C; the key uses $5\ \text{m}$ from that line to B. The result is unaffected because the couple is a free vector and the vertical leg adds no moment.

Example 8 · Reactions on Member ABC with Roller, Hinge, and Pulley Loads

Source: Individual Homework 6, Q3 (Canvas 20760; key Solution-HW6, Canvas 8480, Fall 2025). Ch. 4, 2D rigid-body equilibrium.

Problem. Member ABC is connected at A to a roller support and at B to a hinged support. Determine the reactions at A and B. (Figure described in words: A is on a $30^\circ$ incline with a $60\ \text{cm}$ leg AB; a $300\ \text{N}$ cable tension pulls upward at D; a $200\ \text{N}$ weight hangs from a pulley of radius $10\ \text{cm}$ at C. Horizontal distances from B: $25\ \text{cm}$ to D, a further $25\ \text{cm}$ to E, a further $30\ \text{cm}$ to C.)

Strategy. The roller at A pushes perpendicular to its surface (one unknown $F_A$); the hinge gives $B_x,B_y$. Take moments about B so the hinge forces drop out, then use the two force equations. Per the key's FBD, $F_A$ is perpendicular to AB (which makes $60^\circ$ with the horizontal), acts down and to the left, and has a $60\ \text{cm}$ lever arm. The $200\ \text{N}$ cable leaves the right side of the pulley, so its arm is $25+25+30+10=90\ \text{cm}$.

Key equations. (Arms in cm, so the moment equation is in N·cm.)

$$\sum M_B=0:\ 60F_A+300(25)-200(90)=0\Rightarrow F_A=\frac{18000-7500}{60}=175\ \text{N}$$ $$\sum F_x=0:\ B_x-175\cos60^\circ=0\Rightarrow B_x=87.5\ \text{N}$$ $$\sum F_y=0:\ B_y-175\sin60^\circ-200+300=0\Rightarrow B_y=51.55\ \text{N}$$
$$\boxed{F_A=175\ \text{N}}\qquad\boxed{B_x=87.5\ \text{N}\ \rightarrow}\qquad\boxed{B_y=51.55\ \text{N}\ \uparrow}$$

Check. Moments about A (cm from B, $A\approx(-51.96,\,30)$) give $+5.30\times10^{3}$ (hinge), $+2.309\times10^{4}$ ($300\ \text{N}$), $-2.839\times10^{4}$ ($200\ \text{N}$), summing to zero within rounding.

Match to key Matches Solution-HW6. The key is from the Fall 2025 offering (Canvas 8480); its figure values match Fall 2026 HW6 Q3.
