Engineering Statics: Chapters 3-4 Solved Examples

Example 1

A bent rod is fixed to a wall at its left end. Place the origin O at the wall, with x to the right and y upward. The rod runs horizontally for 4\ \mathrm{m} to a bend, then continues for 3\ \mathrm{m} along a line 30^\circ below the horizontal. Four forces act on the rod: a 50\ \mathrm{N} force downward at x=2\ \mathrm{m}; a 60\ \mathrm{N} force in the +x direction at the bend (x=4\ \mathrm{m}); and, at the free end, a 20\ \mathrm{N} force in the +x direction and a 40\ \mathrm{N} force downward.

Find the resultant moment of the four forces about O.

Sign convention. Counterclockwise (CCW) moments are positive. A force produces a moment M=F\,d, where d is the perpendicular distance from O to the line of action. Each line of action may be extended, so d is measured to the extended line.

Geometry of the free end. The end of the rod is at x_e = 4+3\cos30^\circ = 6.598\ \mathrm{m},\qquad y_e = -3\sin30^\circ = -1.5\ \mathrm{m}.

Moment of each force about O.

  • 50\ \mathrm{N} (down, d=2\ \mathrm{m}): the force at x>0 pointing down turns the rod clockwise, so M=-50(2)=-100\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}.

  • 60\ \mathrm{N} (along +x): its line of action is the x-axis and passes through O, so d=0 and M=0.

  • 20\ \mathrm{N} (along +x): the line of action lies 3\sin30^\circ=1.5\ \mathrm{m} below O. A rightward force below the pivot turns the rod CCW, so M=+20(3\sin30^\circ)=+30\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}.

  • 40\ \mathrm{N} (down): the line of action is at x_e=4+3\cos30^\circ from O, so M=-40(4+3\cos30^\circ)=-263.9\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} (clockwise).

Sum of moments. \begin{align*} (M_R)_O &= \sum Fd \\ &= -50(2)+60(0)+20(3\sin30^\circ)-40(4+3\cos30^\circ)\\ &= -100+0+30-263.9 = -333.9\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} \end{align*} The negative sign means the net turning effect is clockwise.

\boxed{(M_R)_O \approx 334\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}\ \text{clockwise}\ (-334\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}\ \text{CCW positive})}

Example 2

Two parallel forces of equal magnitude act on a gear of radius 0.2\ \mathrm{m} with center O. Each force has magnitude F=600\ \mathrm{N} and the forces point in opposite directions. The first acts at the right-hand end of the horizontal diameter and points down and to the left, 30^\circ below the horizontal. The second acts at the bottom end of the vertical diameter and points up and to the right, 30^\circ above the horizontal.

Find the magnitude and direction of the couple moment acting on the gear.

Approach. The two forces are equal, opposite and parallel, so they sum to zero and form a couple. The couple moment is a free vector: its value does not depend on the point about which moments are taken. Take moments about the center O with CCW positive.

Resolve each force into components. F\cos30^\circ = 600\cos30^\circ = 519.6\ \mathrm{N},\qquad F\sin30^\circ = 600\sin30^\circ = 300\ \mathrm{N}.

Moment about O. Each force acts at a distance 0.2\ \mathrm{m} from O along an axis.

  • The force at the right end of the horizontal diameter: its horizontal component passes through O and gives no moment. Its vertical component (300\ \mathrm{N}, downward) acts 0.2\ \mathrm{m} to the right of O and turns the gear clockwise: -(600\sin30^\circ)(0.2).

  • The force at the bottom end of the vertical diameter: its vertical component passes through O and gives no moment. Its horizontal component (519.6\ \mathrm{N}, rightward) acts 0.2\ \mathrm{m} below O and turns the gear counterclockwise: +(600\cos30^\circ)(0.2).

\begin{align*} M &= \sum M_O = (600\cos30^\circ)(0.2)-(600\sin30^\circ)(0.2)\\ &= 103.9-60.0 = 43.9\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} \end{align*}

Check. Taking moments about a different point, such as the point where the first force is applied, gives the same 43.9\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}. Equivalently, M=Fd with perpendicular separation d=M/F=0.0732\ \mathrm{m} between the two lines of action. The positive sign confirms a counterclockwise sense.

\boxed{M = 43.9\ \mathrm{N\cdot m},\ \text{counterclockwise (directed out of the page)}}

Example 3

A horizontal member is fixed to a wall at the left end O, with x to the right and y upward. The loads on it are:

  • a 750\ \mathrm{N} downward force at 1.25\ \mathrm{m} from O;

  • a 500\ \mathrm{N} force applied at x=2.5\ \mathrm{m} at the top of a short vertical post, 1\ \mathrm{m} above the member, directed up and to the right along a 3–4–5 slope (horizontal : vertical : hypotenuse =3:4:5);

  • at the right end, two horizontal 200\ \mathrm{N} forces on a vertical post, 1\ \mathrm{m} apart: the upper one points left and the lower one points right.

Find the equivalent resultant force (magnitude and direction) and the couple moment acting at O.

Force summation. The two 200\ \mathrm{N} forces are equal and opposite, so their vector sum is zero and they contribute only a couple. The 500\ \mathrm{N} force has components \tfrac35(500) horizontally and \tfrac45(500) vertically. \begin{align*} (F_R)_x &= \sum F_x = \tfrac35(500) = 300\ \mathrm{N}\ (\rightarrow)\\ (F_R)_y &= \sum F_y = \tfrac45(500)-750 = -350\ \mathrm{N} = 350\ \mathrm{N}\ (\downarrow) \end{align*} \begin{align*} F_R &= \sqrt{(F_R)_x^2+(F_R)_y^2} = \sqrt{300^2+350^2} = 461.0\ \mathrm{N}\\ \theta &= \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{350}{300}\right) = 49.4^\circ \quad\text{(below the $+x$ axis)} \end{align*}

Moment summation about O (CCW positive). The couple of the two 200\ \mathrm{N} forces is 200(1)=200\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} counterclockwise (the upper force points left and the lower points right), and it may be added directly because a couple moment is a free vector. For the 500\ \mathrm{N} force, the vertical component 400\ \mathrm{N} acts 2.5\ \mathrm{m} from O (CCW) and the horizontal component 300\ \mathrm{N} acts 1\ \mathrm{m} above O (CW). \begin{align*} (M_R)_O &= \sum M_O + \sum M\\ &= 400(2.5)-300(1)-750(1.25)+200\\ &= 1000-300-937.5+200 = -37.5\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} \end{align*} The negative sign indicates a clockwise couple.

\boxed{\begin{aligned} F_R&=461\ \mathrm{N},\quad\theta=49.4^\circ\ \text{below }+x\\ (M_R)_O&=37.5\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}\ \text{clockwise}\end{aligned}} Components: (F_R)_x = 300\ \mathrm{N}\rightarrow, (F_R)_y = 350\ \mathrm{N}\downarrow, both acting at O.

Example 4

A horizontal beam (weight neglected) has a rocker support at its left end A and a pin support at its right end B, with A and B 7\ \mathrm{m} apart. The pin at B lies on the beam centerline. The loads are:

  • a 600\ \mathrm{N} force pointing down and to the right at 45^\circ below the horizontal, applied 2\ \mathrm{m} from A at a point 0.2\ \mathrm{m} above the beam centerline;

  • a 100\ \mathrm{N} downward force at point D, 3\ \mathrm{m} to the right of the 600\ \mathrm{N} force’s point of application (5\ \mathrm{m} from A);

  • a 200\ \mathrm{N} downward force at B, 2\ \mathrm{m} to the right of D.

Find the horizontal and vertical components of the reaction at the pin B and the vertical reaction of the rocker A.

Free-body diagram. The rocker can push only perpendicular to its surface, so it supplies a vertical force A_y (upward). The pin supplies B_x and B_y; assume B_x points left and B_y points up. The 600\ \mathrm{N} force is replaced by components 600\cos45^\circ = 424.26\ \mathrm{N}\ (\rightarrow),\qquad 600\sin45^\circ = 424.26\ \mathrm{N}\ (\downarrow).

Equilibrium. Sum forces in x (right positive): \sum F_x=0:\quad 600\cos45^\circ - B_x = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; B_x = 424.26\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(leftward, as assumed)}. Sum moments about B (CCW positive) to eliminate B_x and B_y. The downward forces left of B turn the beam CCW about B; the 0.2\ \mathrm{m} offset of the horizontal component gives a clockwise term, and A_y (upward, left of B) is clockwise: \begin{align*} \sum M_B = 0:\quad & 100(2)+(600\sin45^\circ)(5)-(600\cos45^\circ)(0.2)-A_y(7)=0\\ & 200+2121.3-84.85-7A_y=0\\ & A_y = \frac{2236.47}{7} = 319.495\ \mathrm{N}\approx 319\ \mathrm{N} \end{align*} Sum forces in y (up positive), using the unrounded A_y: \begin{align*} \sum F_y=0:\quad & A_y - 600\sin45^\circ-100-200+B_y=0\\ & B_y = 424.26+300-319.495 = 404.77\ \mathrm{N}\approx 405\ \mathrm{N} \end{align*}

Check. Summing moments about A with the computed reactions, and also about D, gives zero, as do \sum F_x and \sum F_y. All three reactions came out positive, matching the assumed directions.

\boxed{B_x = 424\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(left)},\quad A_y = 319\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(up)},\quad B_y = 405\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(up)}}
