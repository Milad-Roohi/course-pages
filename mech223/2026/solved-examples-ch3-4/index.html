1. Moment of a single force about a point

Level: easy. Chapter 3. Target confusion: a moment depends on which point you take it about.

Given / Find

A bracket is pinned to a wall at $A$. A cable pulls on the bracket at $B$, which is $0.400~\text{m}$ to the right of and $0.300~\text{m}$ above $A$. The cable force is $\mathbf F=(150\,\mathbf i-200\,\mathbf j)~\text{N}$.

Find: (a) the moment of $\mathbf F$ about $A$; (b) the moment of the same force about point $C$, which is $0.400~\text{m}$ to the right of $A$ on the same horizontal line as $A$.

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
Bracket pinned to a wall at $A$. Point $B$ is $0.400~\text{m}$ right and $0.300~\text{m}$ above $A$. A $250~\text{N}$ cable force acts at $B$, pointing right and down with components $150~\text{N}$ (right) and $200~\text{N}$ (down). Point $C$ lies $0.400~\text{m}$ right of $A$ on the line through $A$.
Free-body diagram: the cable force is shown as components $F_x=150$ N and $F_y=200$ N at $B$. The unknown pin reactions $A_x$, $A_y$ pass through $A$, so they contribute zero moment about $A$. Position vector $\mathbf r_{B/A}$ runs from $A$ to $B$.

Strategy

The moment of a force about a point is $\mathbf M_O=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F$, where $\mathbf r$ runs from the point to any point on the line of action of the force. In 2D this is $M=xF_y-yF_x$ with $(x,y)$ the components of $\mathbf r$. We will compute it, then verify with $M=Fd$ using the perpendicular distance. Repeating with a second point shows the result changes with the point.

Solution

(a) About $A$. $\mathbf r_{B/A}=(0.400\,\mathbf i+0.300\,\mathbf j)~\text{m}$.

$$\begin{aligned} M_A &= x F_y - y F_x = (0.400)(-200) - (0.300)(150)\\ &= -80.0 - 45.0 = -125~\text{N·m}. \end{aligned}$$

The negative sign means clockwise.

Check with $M=Fd$. The unit vector along $\mathbf F$ is $\mathbf u=(0.6,-0.8)$. The perpendicular distance from $A$ to the line of action is $d=|\mathbf r\times\mathbf u|=|(0.400)(-0.8)-(0.300)(0.6)|=0.500~\text{m}$. Then $|M_A|=Fd=(250)(0.500)=125~\text{N·m}$. ✓

(b) About $C$. $\mathbf r_{B/C}=(0\,\mathbf i+0.300\,\mathbf j)~\text{m}$.

$$M_C = (0)(-200) - (0.300)(150) = -45.0~\text{N·m}.$$

Here only $F_x$ contributes, since $F_y$ acts along the vertical line through $C$.

Answer

$$\boxed{M_A = 125~\text{N·m clockwise}}\qquad \boxed{M_C = 45.0~\text{N·m clockwise}}$$

Sanity check. The same force gives two different moments because the moment arm changed ($0.500~\text{m}$ about $A$, $0.180~\text{m}$ about $C$, since $45.0/250=0.180$). Both are clockwise, which matches the picture: the force pushes $B$ down and to the right, rotating the bracket clockwise about either point.

2. Couple versus moment of a force

Level: easy–medium. Chapter 3. Target confusion: the moment of a force depends on the point; the moment of a couple does not.

Given / Find

Two vertical forces act on a plate. $\mathbf F_1=+120~\text{N}\,\mathbf j$ acts at $D=(0.600,\,0.200)~\text{m}$ and $\mathbf F_2=-120~\text{N}\,\mathbf j$ acts at $E=(0.150,\,0.350)~\text{m}$, with coordinates measured from the origin $O$. Let $G=(0.300,\,-0.100)~\text{m}$ be another reference point.

Find: (a) the net force and net moment of the pair about $O$; (b) the net moment of the pair about $G$; (c) the moment of $\mathbf F_1$ alone about $O$ and about $G$.

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
A plate with two parallel, equal, opposite vertical forces of $120~\text{N}$ each. $F_1$ acts upward at $D$ and $F_2$ acts downward at $E$. The two lines of action are $0.450~\text{m}$ apart horizontally. $O$ is the origin and $G$ is a second reference point below the plate center.
Free-body diagram: only the two applied forces $\mathbf F_1$ and $\mathbf F_2=-\mathbf F_1$ are shown, with the position vectors $\mathbf r_{D/O}$, $\mathbf r_{E/O}$ (from $O$) and $\mathbf r_{D/G}$, $\mathbf r_{E/G}$ (from $G$).

Strategy

Compute $\sum\mathbf M=\sum\mathbf r\times\mathbf F$ about $O$ and then about $G$, and compare. Because the forces are equal and opposite, the position vectors should combine into the single vector $\mathbf r_{D/E}$ and the reference point should drop out. Contrast this with one force alone, whose moment changes with the point.

Solution

(a) Net force: $\mathbf F_1+\mathbf F_2=\mathbf 0$. About $O$, with $M=xF_y-yF_x$ and $F_x=0$:

$$\begin{aligned} M_O &= x_D F_{1y} + x_E F_{2y} = (0.600)(120) + (0.150)(-120)\\ &= 72.0 - 18.0 = +54.0~\text{N·m}\ (\text{CCW}). \end{aligned}$$

(b) About $G$: $\mathbf r_{D/G}=(0.300,\,0.300)$ m and $\mathbf r_{E/G}=(-0.150,\,0.450)$ m.

$$M_G = (0.300)(120) + (-0.150)(-120) = 36.0 + 18.0 = +54.0~\text{N·m}.$$

General reason. For any point $P$,

$$\mathbf M_P=\mathbf r_{D/P}\times\mathbf F_1+\mathbf r_{E/P}\times(-\mathbf F_1)=(\mathbf r_{D/P}-\mathbf r_{E/P})\times\mathbf F_1=\mathbf r_{D/E}\times\mathbf F_1,$$

which does not contain $P$. Also $|M|=Fd=(120)(0.450)=54.0~\text{N·m}$, with $d$ the perpendicular distance between the two lines of action.

(c) Force $\mathbf F_1$ alone:

$$M_O^{(F_1)} = (0.600)(120) = +72.0~\text{N·m},\qquad M_G^{(F_1)} = (0.300)(120) = +36.0~\text{N·m}.$$
About $O$About $G$
$\mathbf F_1$ alone$+72.0$ N·m$+36.0$ N·m
$\mathbf F_2$ alone$-18.0$ N·m$+18.0$ N·m
Pair (couple)$+54.0$ N·m$+54.0$ N·m

Answer

$$\boxed{\mathbf F_{\text{net}}=\mathbf 0,\quad M_O=M_G=54.0~\text{N·m CCW}}$$

$\mathbf F_1$ alone: $72.0~\text{N·m}$ about $O$ and $36.0~\text{N·m}$ about $G$ (both CCW).

Sanity check. The couple moment equals force $\times$ perpendicular separation ($120\times0.450=54.0$ N·m) no matter where you compute it, so it is a free vector. A single force has no such property; its moment changed from $72.0$ to $36.0$ N·m when only the reference point moved.

3. Equivalent force–couple system

Level: medium. Chapter 3. HW6-style equivalent system.

Given / Find

A horizontal bar has $A$ at its left end. Loads act on it as follows (positions measured from $A$):

  • $\mathbf F_1=(0,\,-300)~\text{N}$ at $(0.500,\,0)~\text{m}$;
  • $\mathbf F_2=(160,\,-120)~\text{N}$ at $(1.20,\,0.300)~\text{m}$ (on a short bracket above the bar);
  • $\mathbf F_3=(0,\,+180)~\text{N}$ at $(2.00,\,0)~\text{m}$;
  • a pure couple $C=90.0~\text{N·m}$, clockwise.

Find: (a) the equivalent force–couple system at $A$; (b) the equivalent system at $B$, the right end at $(2.00,\,0)$; (c) the point on the bar where the resultant force alone, with no couple, is equivalent to the original system.

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
Horizontal bar from $A$ (left) to $B$ (right, $2.00~\text{m}$ from $A$). $F_1=300~\text{N}$ acts downward $0.500~\text{m}$ from $A$. $F_2=200~\text{N}$ (components $160~\text{N}$ right, $120~\text{N}$ down) acts at the top of a bracket $1.20~\text{m}$ from $A$ and $0.300~\text{m}$ above the bar. $F_3=180~\text{N}$ acts upward at $B$. A $90.0~\text{N·m}$ clockwise couple also acts on the bar. Horizontal dimensions are $0.500$, $0.700$ and $0.800~\text{m}$ in sequence.
Equivalent system at $A$: the resultant $\mathbf R$ (components $R_x=160~\text{N}$ right and $R_y=240~\text{N}$ down, magnitude $288~\text{N}$) acts at $A$ together with a clockwise couple $M_A=72.0~\text{N·m}$.

Strategy

Two systems are equivalent if they have the same resultant force and the same total moment about one point (and hence about all points). So: sum the forces to get $\mathbf R$; sum the moments of every force and the couple about $A$ to get $M_A$. Couples add directly to the moment sum without needing a position vector. To move the system to another point, use $\mathbf M_B=\mathbf M_A+\mathbf r_{A/B}\times\mathbf R$.

Solution

Resultant force.

$$\begin{aligned} R_x &= 0 + 160 + 0 = 160~\text{N},\\ R_y &= -300 - 120 + 180 = -240~\text{N},\\ R &= \sqrt{160^2+240^2}=288~\text{N},\qquad \theta=\tan^{-1}\frac{240}{160}=56.3^\circ \text{ below the } +x\text{-axis}. \end{aligned}$$

Moment about $A$ ($M=xF_y-yF_x$, CCW positive):

$$\begin{aligned} M_{1}&=(0.500)(-300) - 0 = -150~\text{N·m},\\ M_{2}&=(1.20)(-120)-(0.300)(160) = -144-48.0=-192~\text{N·m},\\ M_{3}&=(2.00)(180)-0 = +360~\text{N·m},\\ M_{C}&=-90.0~\text{N·m}\quad(\text{couple, clockwise}). \end{aligned}$$ $$M_A=-150-192+360-90.0=-72.0~\text{N·m}\quad(\text{clockwise}).$$

(b) Move to $B$. $\mathbf r_{A/B}=(-2.00,\,0)$ m, so $\mathbf r_{A/B}\times\mathbf R = (-2.00)(-240)-0=+480~\text{N·m}$:

$$M_B=-72.0+480=+408~\text{N·m}\ (\text{CCW}),\qquad \mathbf R\text{ unchanged}.$$

(c) Single force with no couple. Place $\mathbf R$ at $(x,0)$ on the bar so its moment about $A$ equals $M_A$:

$$x\,R_y=M_A\;\Rightarrow\; x(-240)=-72.0\;\Rightarrow\; x=0.300~\text{m}.$$

Answer

$$\boxed{\text{At }A:\ \mathbf R=(160\,\mathbf i-240\,\mathbf j)~\text{N}\ (288~\text{N at }56.3^\circ\text{ below }+x),\ \ M_A=72.0~\text{N·m clockwise}}$$ $$\boxed{\text{At }B:\ \mathbf R\text{ same},\ M_B=408~\text{N·m CCW}}\qquad \boxed{\text{Single resultant: }\mathbf R\text{ acting at }x=0.300~\text{m from }A}$$

Sanity check. Placing $\mathbf R$ alone at $x=0.300$ m gives moment $0.300\times(-240)=-72.0$ N·m about $A$, matching $M_A$, and moment $(0.300-2.00)(-240)=+408$ N·m about $B$, matching part (b). The two descriptions agree at both points, as equivalent systems must.

4. Simply supported beam: pin, roller, a load and a couple

Level: medium. Chapter 4. Target confusion: why a pin gives two reaction components and a roller only one, and how the moment center removes unknowns.

Given / Find

A horizontal beam $AB$ is $4.00~\text{m}$ long. It is held by a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$ that rests on a horizontal surface. A $500~\text{N}$ force with components $300~\text{N}$ to the right and $400~\text{N}$ downward acts on the beam $1.50~\text{m}$ from $A$. A $400~\text{N·m}$ counterclockwise couple is applied to the beam at $3.00~\text{m}$ from $A$. Treat the beam weight as negligible.

Find: the support reactions at $A$ and $B$.

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
Beam $AB$, $4.00~\text{m}$ long, on a pin at $A$ (left) and a roller at $B$ (right, free to roll horizontally). The $500~\text{N}$ force acts $1.50~\text{m}$ from $A$ and points right and down, with components $300~\text{N}$ and $400~\text{N}$. A $400~\text{N·m}$ counterclockwise couple acts on the beam $3.00~\text{m}$ from $A$. Horizontal dimensions are $1.50$, $1.50$ and $1.00~\text{m}$ in sequence.
Free-body diagram: supports removed. Assumed reactions: $A_x$ to the right and $A_y$ upward at the pin, and only a vertical $B_y$ at the roller. The solution will show that $A_x$ comes out negative, i.e. it really points left.

Strategy

Reactions come from what the support blocks.

  • Pin at $A$. It stops the beam point from moving in any direction in the plane, so it can push or pull both horizontally and vertically: two unknowns, $A_x$ and $A_y$.
  • Roller at $B$. It stops motion only into or away from the surface it sits on, and lets the beam slide along it. The surface is horizontal, so the reaction is vertical only: one unknown, $B_y$. There is no horizontal reaction at $B$.

That is three unknowns for three planar equations ($\sum F_x=0$, $\sum F_y=0$, $\sum M=0$), so the beam is statically determinate.

Choose the moment center to kill unknowns. Taking moments about $A$ removes $A_x$ and $A_y$, since both pass through $A$. That leaves $B_y$ as the only unknown in one equation. The $300~\text{N}$ component acts along the beam axis through $A$, so it has no moment about $A$. The couple enters the moment sum as its full value $+400~\text{N·m}$, wherever on the beam it is applied (a couple is a free vector, as in Example 2). Then use the force equations for $A_x$ and $A_y$, and check with a moment sum about $B$.

Solution

Moments about $A$ (CCW positive; only the vertical $400~\text{N}$ component, $B_y$ and the couple contribute):

$$\begin{aligned} \sum M_A &= B_y(4.00) - (400)(1.50) + 400 = 0\\ 4.00\,B_y &= 600 - 400 = 200 \;\Rightarrow\; B_y = +50.0~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

Horizontal forces:

$$\sum F_x = A_x + 300 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = -300~\text{N}.$$

The negative sign means the pin pushes the beam to the left.

Vertical forces:

$$\sum F_y = A_y + B_y - 400 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 400 - 50.0 = +350~\text{N}.$$

Pin reaction as one vector: $A=\sqrt{300^2+350^2}=461~\text{N}$, directed $\tan^{-1}(350/300)=49.4^\circ$ above the $-x$ direction (up and to the left).

Independent check, moments about $B$. Now $B_y$ drops out. With $r$ measured from $B$:

$$\sum M_B = -A_y(4.00) + (400)(2.50) + 400 = -1400 + 1000 + 400 = 0.\ \checkmark$$

Answer

$$\boxed{A_x = 300~\text{N (to the left)}},\qquad \boxed{A_y = 350~\text{N (up)}},\qquad \boxed{B_y = 50.0~\text{N (up)}}$$

Equivalently the pin force is $461~\text{N}$ at $49.4^\circ$ above the $-x$ axis. There is no horizontal force at the roller.

Sanity check. Net horizontal: the pin's $300~\text{N}$ to the left cancels the applied $300~\text{N}$ to the right. Net vertical: $350+50.0=400~\text{N}$ up balances the $400~\text{N}$ down. Physically, the downward load alone would give a $600~\text{N·m}$ clockwise moment about $A$ that $B_y$ must resist, but the counterclockwise couple already supplies $400~\text{N·m}$ of it, which is why $B_y$ is small. Because $B_y>0$ the roller is being pushed on, as a roller requires. If a calculation had produced a negative $B_y$, the roller would have to pull on the beam, which it cannot do, and the beam would lift off.

5. L-shaped bracket with a pin and a wall roller

Level: medium–hard. Chapter 4. Target confusion: the free-body-diagram workflow when the supports are of different types and the roller is on a vertical surface.

Given / Find

An L-shaped bracket is mounted against a vertical wall. Its vertical arm is $1.20~\text{m}$ tall and its horizontal arm sticks out $1.50~\text{m}$ from the wall. The bracket is held by a pin at $A$ at the top of the vertical arm and a roller at $C$ at the bottom corner, where the wheel rolls on the wall. A $500~\text{N}$ force acts at the end $D$ of the horizontal arm, with components $300~\text{N}$ to the right (away from the wall) and $400~\text{N}$ downward. Neglect the bracket weight.

Find: all the support reactions at $A$ and $C$.

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
L-shaped bracket against a vertical wall (left). The pin $A$ joins the top of the vertical arm to the wall. The roller $C$ is at the bottom corner, a wheel touching the wall. The vertical arm is $1.20~\text{m}$ long and the horizontal arm $1.50~\text{m}$. A $500~\text{N}$ force acts at the free end $D$, pointing right and down with components $300~\text{N}$ (away from the wall) and $400~\text{N}$ (down).
Free-body diagram: wall removed. The pin at $A$ becomes two components $A_x$ (assumed right) and $A_y$ (assumed up). The roller at $C$ rolls on a vertical wall, so it can only push horizontally: a single $C_x$, assumed to the right (away from the wall, the only direction a roller can push). The applied force is shown with its components.

Strategy

A reliable workflow for any support mix:

  1. Isolate the bracket and sketch only the bracket.
  2. Replace each support by what it blocks. Pin → two components ($A_x,A_y$). Roller on a vertical wall → one horizontal force ($C_x$), because the wheel is free to roll up and down the wall but cannot move into it or away from it.
  3. Draw the known load with its components, and count: three unknowns ($A_x$, $A_y$, $C_x$), three equations.
  4. Moment center at the pin $A$. Both $A_x$ and $A_y$ pass through $A$, so $\sum M_A=0$ contains only $C_x$ and the applied force.
  5. Force sums give $A_x$ and $A_y$.
  6. Check with a moment sum about a different point, here $C$.

Use $M=xF_y-yF_x$ with the position vector measured from the moment center to the point where the force acts. Place the origin at $C$: then $A=(0,\,1.20)$ m, $C=(0,\,0)$ and $D=(1.50,\,0)$ m.

Solution

Moments about $A$. Here $\mathbf r_{C/A}=(0,\,-1.20)$ m and $\mathbf r_{D/A}=(1.50,\,-1.20)$ m.

$$\begin{aligned} M_{C_x} &= x F_y - y F_x = (0)(0) - (-1.20)(C_x) = +1.20\,C_x,\\ M_{F} &= (1.50)(-400) - (-1.20)(300) = -600 + 360 = -240~\text{N·m}. \end{aligned}$$ $$\sum M_A = 1.20\,C_x - 240 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; C_x = +200~\text{N}.$$

The positive sign agrees with the assumed direction: the wheel pushes away from the wall, as it must.

Horizontal and vertical forces:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= A_x + C_x + 300 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = -200 - 300 = -500~\text{N},\\ \sum F_y &= A_y - 400 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = +400~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

The pin pulls the bracket toward the wall with $500~\text{N}$ and holds it up with $400~\text{N}$. As one vector, $A=\sqrt{500^2+400^2}=640~\text{N}$, directed $\tan^{-1}(400/500)=38.7^\circ$ above the $-x$ direction.

Independent check, moments about $C$. With $\mathbf r_{A/C}=(0,\,1.20)$ m and $\mathbf r_{D/C}=(1.50,\,0)$ m:

$$\sum M_C = \underbrace{-(1.20)(A_x)}_{+600} + \underbrace{(0)(A_y)}_{0} + \underbrace{(1.50)(-400)}_{-600} = 0.\ \checkmark$$

Answer

$$\boxed{C_x = 200~\text{N (to the right, away from the wall)}}$$ $$\boxed{A_x = 500~\text{N (to the left)}},\qquad \boxed{A_y = 400~\text{N (up)}}\quad(A=640~\text{N at }38.7^\circ\text{ above }-x).$$

Sanity check. The horizontal forces balance: $-500+200+300=0$. The $500~\text{N}$ force at $D$ tries to rotate the bracket clockwise about the wall, pulling the top away from the wall and pushing the bottom in. The wall's reactions oppose this: the pin pulls the top in and the roller pushes the bottom out. Taken about $A$, the applied force has a net $240~\text{N·m}$ clockwise moment, and $C_x=200~\text{N}$ acting $1.20~\text{m}$ below $A$ supplies the $240~\text{N·m}$ counterclockwise moment that balances it. Also, $C_x$ had to be positive; a negative value would have meant the wheel was being pulled into the wall, which a roller cannot do.

6. Rigid beam on a pin and an inclined roller

Level: medium–hard. Chapter 4. Target confusion: the direction of a reaction is set by what the support blocks, not by whatever direction looks convenient.

Given / Find

A rigid horizontal beam $AB$ is $3.00~\text{m}$ long. It is held by a pin at $A$ and by a frictionless roller at $B$ that rests on a smooth incline. The incline rises $3$ units for every $4$ horizontal units ($\theta=36.9^\circ$ above the horizontal), going up toward the right. The beam carries a $800~\text{N}$ weight acting at $1.20~\text{m}$ from $A$ (the load, with the beam's own weight included).

Find: the reaction at the roller $B$ (magnitude and direction) and the pin reaction components at $A$.

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
Horizontal beam $AB$, $3.00~\text{m}$ long, pinned at $A$ (left) and resting on a roller at $B$ (right). The roller sits on a smooth incline that rises $3$ units vertically per $4$ horizontally, so the surface makes an angle of $36.9^\circ$ with the horizontal. An $800~\text{N}$ weight acts downward $1.20~\text{m}$ from $A$, and $B$ is $1.80~\text{m}$ beyond it.
Free-body diagram: at the pin, assumed components $A_x$ (right) and $A_y$ (up). At the roller, a single force $N_B$ perpendicular to the incline, pointing away from it (up and to the left), along the unit normal $\hat{\mathbf n}=(-0.6,\,0.8)$, which is perpendicular to the incline direction $(0.8,\,0.6)$.

Strategy

What each support blocks.

  • Pin at $A$: blocks translation in both directions, so two components $A_x$, $A_y$.
  • Roller at $B$ on a smooth incline: blocks motion into the incline and nothing along it, because the wheel rolls freely along the surface and there is no friction. So the reaction is purely perpendicular to the incline: one unknown magnitude $N_B$ along a known direction. It is not vertical, and it has a horizontal component.

The incline direction is $(4,3)/5=(0.8,\,0.6)$. A perpendicular unit vector is $(-3,4)/5=(-0.6,\,0.8)$, chosen to point away from the solid incline (a roller can push, not pull). Thus

$$\mathbf N_B = N_B\,(-0.6\,\mathbf i + 0.8\,\mathbf j).$$

The unknowns are $A_x$, $A_y$, $N_B$: three unknowns, three equations. Moments about $A$ remove $A_x$ and $A_y$ and leave only $N_B$, so solve for $N_B$ first, then use the force sums.

Solution

Moments about $A$. $B$ is at $\mathbf r_{B/A}=(3.00,\,0)$ m. The force $N_B$ has components $(-0.6N_B,\;0.8N_B)$. Its horizontal component acts along the beam line and has no moment; only the vertical component does. The weight acts at $x=1.20$ m.

$$\begin{aligned} \sum M_A &= (3.00)(0.8\,N_B) - (1.20)(800) = 0\\ 2.40\,N_B &= 960 \;\Rightarrow\; N_B = 400~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

So the components at $B$ are $N_{Bx}=-0.6(400)=-240~\text{N}$ and $N_{By}=0.8(400)=+320~\text{N}$.

Force sums:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= A_x + N_{Bx} = A_x - 240 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = +240~\text{N},\\ \sum F_y &= A_y + N_{By} - 800 = A_y + 320 - 800 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = +480~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

The pin force is $A=\sqrt{240^2+480^2}=537~\text{N}$ at $\tan^{-1}(480/240)=63.4^\circ$ above the $+x$ axis.

Independent check, moments about $B$. Now $A_x$ and $N_B$ drop out (their lines pass through $B$ or along the beam):

$$\sum M_B = -(3.00)(A_y) + (1.80)(800) = -1440 + 1440 = 0.\ \checkmark$$

What if the roller is drawn vertical? If we pretended $B$ sat on a flat floor, we would take $B_y$ vertical: $\sum M_A$ gives $3.00B_y=960$, so $B_y=320~\text{N}$ and $\sum F_x$ gives $A_x=0$. Both are wrong. The incline pushes the beam toward the left with $240~\text{N}$, and the pin must push back to the right with $240~\text{N}$.

Answer

$$\boxed{N_B = 400~\text{N, perpendicular to the incline, up and to the left}}\quad(N_{Bx}=-240~\text{N},\ N_{By}=+320~\text{N})$$ $$\boxed{A_x = 240~\text{N (to the right)}},\qquad \boxed{A_y = 480~\text{N (up)}}\quad(A=537~\text{N at }63.4^\circ\text{ above }+x).$$

The normal direction makes an angle of $36.9^\circ$ with the vertical, the same as the incline angle with the horizontal.

Sanity check. Vertical: $480+320=800~\text{N}$, matching the weight. Horizontal: the incline's $240~\text{N}$ push to the left is cancelled by the pin's $240~\text{N}$ to the right. Reasonableness: the weight is $40\%$ of the way along the beam, so a straight-up support at $B$ would carry about $40\%$ of $800$, i.e. $320~\text{N}$, and the vertical component of $N_B$ is exactly that. The extra horizontal part arises purely because the roller's reaction is tilted by the incline. As required, $N_B>0$ (pushing away from the incline surface).

7. Boom with a pulley, a rope and a support cable

Level: exam-level. Chapter 4. Target confusion: a rope over an ideal pulley pulls with the same tension on both sides, and when the pulley is bolted to the body, both rope segments act on that body.

Given / Find

A horizontal boom $AB$ is $4.00~\text{m}$ long and is pinned to a wall at $A$. Its own weight is negligible. A frictionless pulley of negligible radius is mounted at the free end $B$. A rope is tied to the wall at point $E$, which is $3.00~\text{m}$ below $A$. The rope runs up to the pulley at $B$, passes over it, and carries a hanging weight $W=450~\text{N}$ on its other end, which hangs at rest. A separate support cable runs from point $D$ on the boom, $3.00~\text{m}$ from $A$, to a wall point $C$ that is $4.00~\text{m}$ above $A$.

Find: (a) the tension $T$ in the support cable $DC$; (b) the pin reaction at $A$ (components, magnitude and direction).

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
A horizontal boom $AB$, $4.00~\text{m}$ long, pinned to a wall at $A$ (left). A frictionless pulley is mounted at the free end $B$. A rope is tied to the wall at $E$, $3.00~\text{m}$ below $A$, runs up to the pulley, passes over it, and hangs straight down carrying a $450~\text{N}$ weight. A support cable joins $D$ on the boom ($3.00~\text{m}$ from $A$) to $C$ on the wall, $4.00~\text{m}$ above $A$.
Free-body diagram: boom and pulley together. The cable at $D$ gives one force $T$ along $DC$ (up and toward the wall). Because the pulley is part of the body, both rope segments are drawn at $B$: one pulls straight down with $450~\text{N}$ (the hanging side) and the other pulls toward $E$ (down and toward the wall) with the same $450~\text{N}$. Unknowns: $T$, $A_x$, $A_y$.

Strategy

Ideal pulley. The hanging weight is at rest, so the rope tension on that side equals $W=450~\text{N}$. A frictionless pulley changes the rope's direction but not its tension, so the segment running to $E$ also carries $450~\text{N}$. This is a known force, not an unknown.

Choice of body. Take the boom and pulley as one rigid body. The rope touches the pulley on two sides, so two rope forces act at $B$. Each pulls along the rope, away from the body. The support cable pulls along $DC$. The pin gives $A_x$ and $A_y$. The unknowns are $T$, $A_x$, $A_y$: three unknowns, three equations.

Directions. From $D(3,0)$ to $C(0,4)$ the vector is $(-3,4)$, length $5$, so the cable direction is $(-0.6,\,0.8)$. From $B(4,0)$ to $E(0,-3)$ the vector is $(-4,-3)$, length $5$, so the rope direction is $(-0.8,\,-0.6)$.

Order. Moments about $A$ eliminate $A_x$ and $A_y$ and give $T$ at once. Then the force sums give $A_x$ and $A_y$.

Solution

Forces at the pulley. Hanging segment: $(0,\,-450)$ N. Segment toward $E$: $450\,(-0.8,\,-0.6)=(-360,\,-270)$ N. Both act at $B$.

Moments about $A$ (use $M=xF_y-yF_x$; every force acts on the line $y=0$, so only vertical components matter):

$$\begin{aligned} \sum M_A &= (3.00)(0.8\,T) + (4.00)(-270) + (4.00)(-450) = 0\\ 2.40\,T &= 1080 + 1800 = 2880 \;\Rightarrow\; T = 1200~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

Force sums. The cable force is $T(-0.6,\,0.8)=(-720,\,+960)$ N.

$$\begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= A_x - 720 - 360 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = +1080~\text{N},\\ \sum F_y &= A_y + 960 - 270 - 450 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = -240~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

The negative sign means $A_y$ acts downward, opposite to the arrow assumed on the FBD. Then

$$A=\sqrt{1080^2+240^2}=1106~\text{N},\qquad \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{240}{1080}\right)=12.5^\circ\ \text{below the }+x\text{ axis}.$$

Independent check, moments about $B$. Now $A_x$ and the three forces at $B$ drop out, leaving $A_y$ at lever arm $4.00~\text{m}$ (to the left) and $T$ at $1.00~\text{m}$ (to the left):

$$\sum M_B = (-4.00)(A_y) + (-1.00)(960) = (-4.00)(-240) - 960 = 960 - 960 = 0.\ \checkmark$$

Common mistake: leaving out the rope to $E$. If only the weight is drawn at $B$, the moment equation becomes $2.40\,T=1800$, giving $T=750~\text{N}$, which is $450~\text{N}$ too low. The rope runs over the pulley and also pulls on the body, so both segments belong on the FBD.

Answer

$$\boxed{T = 1200~\text{N}}$$ $$\boxed{A_x = 1080~\text{N (to the right)}},\qquad \boxed{A_y = 240~\text{N (downward)}}\quad(A=1106~\text{N at }12.5^\circ\text{ below }+x).$$

Sanity check. Vertical balance: $-240+960-270-450=0$. Horizontal balance: $1080-720-360=0$. The moment check about $B$ used different unknowns and also closed. Reasonableness: the downward pull at $B$ totals $270+450=720~\text{N}$ at a $4.00~\text{m}$ arm, while the cable acts at only $3.00~\text{m}$, so its vertical component must be $720\times 4/3=960~\text{N}$; dividing by the direction factor $0.8$ gives $T=1200~\text{N}$, as found. $A_y$ points down because the cable's upward pull ($960~\text{N}$) exceeds the $720~\text{N}$ of downward rope pull, so the pin must pull the boom down by the difference, $240~\text{N}$.

8. Two-force and three-force members

Level: medium. Chapter 4. Target confusion: a member loaded at exactly two points carries a force along the line joining them, and a member loaded at exactly three points has force lines that meet at one point.

Given / Find

Part (a), two-force member. A horizontal beam $AD$ is pinned to a wall at $A$. A straight link $BC$ is pinned to the beam at $B$ and pinned to the wall at $C$. The link is weightless and carries no load between its pins. The beam is $5.00~\text{m}$ long. Point $B$ is $3.00~\text{m}$ from $A$ along the beam, and $C$ is $4.00~\text{m}$ directly below $A$. A load $P=600~\text{N}$ acts downward at $D$. Find: the force in link $BC$ (say whether it is in tension or compression) and the pin reaction at $A$.

Part (b), three-force member. A uniform bar $AB$ is $4.00~\text{m}$ long and weighs $600~\text{N}$. It is pinned to a wall at $A$ and held horizontal by a cable attached at $B$ and tied to the wall at point $C$, which is $3.00~\text{m}$ above $A$. Find: the point where the three force lines of action meet, the direction of the pin force at $A$, and the magnitudes of the cable tension and the pin force.

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
Part (a). Beam $AD$ ($5.00~\text{m}$) is pinned to the wall at $A$. Link $BC$ is pinned to the beam at $B$, $3.00~\text{m}$ from $A$, and to the wall at $C$, $4.00~\text{m}$ below $A$, so the link rises $3$ to the right for every $4$ up. A $600~\text{N}$ load pulls straight down at $D$, $2.00~\text{m}$ beyond $B$. FBDs: the beam carries $P$ at $D$, pin components $A_x$, $A_y$ at $A$, and one force $F$ at $B$ along the link (drawn as tension). The link has only two forces on it, both of magnitude $F$, equal, opposite and along $BC$.

Part (b). Uniform bar $AB$, $4.00~\text{m}$ long, pinned to the wall at $A$ and held horizontal by a cable from its end $B$ to the wall at $C$, $3.00~\text{m}$ above $A$. The $600~\text{N}$ weight acts at the midpoint $G$, $2.00~\text{m}$ from each end. FBD: three forces on the bar: the weight $W$ (vertical through $G$), the cable tension $T$ at $B$ (along $BC$) and the pin reaction $R_A$ at $A$ (direction unknown at first). The lines of action of $W$ and $T$ cross at $P$, $2.00~\text{m}$ from the wall and $1.50~\text{m}$ above the bar. The line of $R_A$ must also pass through $P$.

Strategy

Two-force member. If a rigid body is in equilibrium with forces at only two points, the two forces must be equal in magnitude, opposite in direction and collinear. The only line that joins the two points is the line through them. So a straight, weightless link that is pinned at both ends and carries no load in between has a force along its own axis, with magnitude $F$ at both ends. That removes the four pin-component unknowns at $B$ and $C$ and replaces them with one number.

Three-force member. Suppose a body is in equilibrium under three forces whose lines of action are not all parallel. Take the point where two of the lines intersect. The moment of those two forces about that point is zero, so the moment of the third must also be zero. A nonzero force has zero moment about a point only if its line of action passes through that point. Hence the three lines are concurrent. (If two of the forces are parallel, the third must be parallel to them too, since the three forces sum to zero.) Concurrency gives the direction of an unknown force for free.

Plan. In (a), use the link direction $(3,4)/5=(0.6,\,0.8)$ to write the link force on the beam with a single unknown, then take moments about $A$. In (b), find the point $P$, take the pin force along $AP$, and close the force triangle.

Solution

Part (a). The force the link exerts on the beam at $B$ lies along $BC$. Assume tension, so the link pulls the beam toward $C$, direction $(-0.6,\,-0.8)$: components $(-0.6F,\,-0.8F)$.

Moments about $A$ (only the vertical components have lever arms along the beam):

$$\begin{aligned} \sum M_A &= (3.00)(-0.8F) + (5.00)(-600) = 0\\ -2.40F &= 3000 \;\Rightarrow\; F = -1250~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

The negative sign means the link is in compression: it pushes the beam at $B$ with $1250~\text{N}$ in the direction $(0.6,\,0.8)$, that is, $(+750,\,+1000)$ N.

Force sums on the beam:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= A_x + 750 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = -750~\text{N},\\ \sum F_y &= A_y + 1000 - 600 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = -400~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

The pin force is $A=\sqrt{750^2+400^2}=850~\text{N}$, pointing left and down at $\tan^{-1}(400/750)=28.1^\circ$ below the $-x$ axis.

Check, moments about $B$: $(-3.00)(A_y)+(2.00)(-600)=(-3.00)(-400)-1200=0.\ \checkmark$

Without the two-force idea, the link would need its own FBD with pin components at both $B$ and $C$: six unknowns and six equations. With it, the beam alone has three unknowns and three equations.

Part (b). The weight acts at the midpoint, so its line is the vertical $x=2.00~\text{m}$. The cable line passes through $B(4,0)$ and $C(0,3)$. At $x=2.00~\text{m}$ it is at height $y=\tfrac{3}{4}(4-2)=1.50~\text{m}$. So the lines meet at $P=(2.00,\;1.50)$ m.

The line of the pin force passes through $A(0,0)$ and $P$, so its direction is $(2.00,\,1.50)/2.50=(0.8,\,0.6)$, which is $36.9^\circ$ above the $+x$ axis. The force must point up and to the right (toward $P$) to balance the weight, so $\mathbf R_A=R\,(0.8,\,0.6)$ with $R>0$. The cable force on the bar is $\mathbf T=T(-0.8,\,0.6)$ and the weight is $(0,-600)$ N.

$$\begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= 0.8R - 0.8T = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; R = T,\\ \sum F_y &= 0.6R + 0.6T - 600 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 1.2\,T = 600 \;\Rightarrow\; T = R = 500~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

The components are $R_x=400$ N (right) and $R_y=300$ N (up).

Independent check, moments about $A$: $(4.00)(0.6\times 500)+(2.00)(-600)=1200-1200=0.\ \checkmark$ The pin force passes through $A$ and contributes nothing.

Why concurrency helped. A pin normally has two unknown components, so the bar has three unknowns. Concurrency fixes the pin force's direction, leaving two unknown magnitudes, and the two force-sum equations settle them without a moment equation.

Answer

Part (a).

$$\boxed{F_{BC} = 1250~\text{N (compression)}},\qquad \boxed{A = 850~\text{N}}\quad(A_x=750~\text{N left},\ A_y=400~\text{N down;}\ 28.1^\circ\text{ below the }-x\text{ axis}).$$

Part (b).

$$\boxed{T = 500~\text{N}},\qquad \boxed{R_A = 500~\text{N at }36.9^\circ\text{ above the }+x\text{ axis}}\quad(R_x=400~\text{N right},\ R_y=300~\text{N up}),$$

with the three lines of action meeting at $P=(2.00,\,1.50)$ m.

Sanity check. (a) Vertical balance on the beam: $-400+1000-600=0$. Horizontal: $-750+750=0$. Notice also that the beam in (a) is itself a three-force member (pin, link, load). The link force line through $B$ in the direction $(0.6,\,0.8)$ meets the load line $x=5.00~\text{m}$ at $y=\tfrac{4}{3}(2.00)=2.67~\text{m}$, and the line from $A$ to $(5.00,\,2.67)$ has slope $0.533=400/750$, the same as the pin force found above. The concurrency test agrees with the algebra. Reasonableness: the load is $5.00~\text{m}$ out and the link's vertical push acts at only $3.00~\text{m}$, so the link force's vertical part must exceed $600~\text{N}$, and $1000~\text{N}$ does.

(b) Vertical: $300+300=600~\text{N}$ matches the weight. Horizontal: $400$ right at $A$ and $400$ left from the cable. Reasonableness: the cable and pin force make equal angles with the vertical, which is why $T=R$, and each carries half the weight in the vertical direction. Both have positive values, consistent with the assumed senses.

9. Bent bar with a pin, a link, a couple and two forces

Level: exam-level. Chapter 4. Target confusion: with several loads on one body, the FBD must show every one of them, a couple adds its moment with no lever arm, and a pinned two-force link counts as one unknown, not two.

Given / Find

A bent bar $ABC$ has a horizontal leg $AB$ of length $4.00~\text{m}$ and a vertical leg $BC$ of length $3.00~\text{m}$ hanging down from $B$. Its weight is negligible. The bar is pinned to a wall at $A$. A straight, weightless link $CE$ is pinned to the bar at $C$ and pinned to the same wall at $E$, which is $6.00~\text{m}$ below $A$. Three loads act on the bar: a downward force of $800~\text{N}$ at the midpoint $G$ of $AB$, a horizontal force of $500~\text{N}$ pointing right at $C$, and a couple of magnitude $1300~\text{N·m}$ acting counterclockwise on the bar.

Find: (a) the force in link $CE$, and whether it is in tension or compression; (b) the pin reaction at $A$ (components, magnitude and direction).

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
Bent bar $ABC$ pinned to the wall at $A$ (left). Leg $AB$ is $4.00~\text{m}$ long and leg $BC$ drops $3.00~\text{m}$. Link $CE$ joins $C$ to the wall pin at $E$, $6.00~\text{m}$ below $A$. Loads: $800~\text{N}$ down at the midpoint $G$ of $AB$; $500~\text{N}$ to the right at $C$; a $1300~\text{N·m}$ couple shown as a counterclockwise arc.
Free-body diagram: bar alone. Known loads include the couple $M_0$ (a free moment, not tied to any point). Unknowns: pin components $A_x$, $A_y$, and the link force $F$ at $C$, drawn as if the link were in tension (pulling the bar down and toward the wall). The link force lies along $CE$, which falls $3$ for every $4$ it runs to the left.

Strategy

Link. Link $CE$ is weightless, loaded only at its two pins, so it is a two-force member. Its force on the bar acts along $CE$. From $C(4,-3)$ to $E(0,-6)$ the vector is $(-4,-3)$, length $5$, so the unit vector is $(-0.8,\,-0.6)$. The link adds one unknown, $F$.

Body and unknowns. Take the bent bar alone. The unknowns are $A_x$, $A_y$ and $F$: three unknowns, and the planar problem has three equations ($\sum F_x$, $\sum F_y$, $\sum M$).

Moment center. Take moments about $A$. Both pin components pass through $A$ and drop out, so one equation gives $F$. Then the two force sums give $A_x$ and $A_y$.

Couple. A couple contributes the same moment about every point, equal to its own magnitude, with sign from its rotation sense. It appears in $\sum M$ but never in $\sum F_x$ or $\sum F_y$.

Moment of each force about $A$ ($M=xF_y-yF_x$, with $(x,y)$ measured from $A$, CCW positive): the link force at $C(4,-3)$ has components $(-0.8F,\,-0.6F)$; the $800~\text{N}$ load acts at $(2,0)$; the $500~\text{N}$ force acts at $(4,-3)$.

Solution

Moments about $A$:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum M_A &= \underbrace{(4)(-0.6F)-(-3)(-0.8F)}_{\text{link force}} + \underbrace{(2)(-800)}_{800~\text{N load}} + \underbrace{-(-3)(500)}_{500~\text{N force}} + \underbrace{1300}_{\text{couple}} = 0\\ &= -4.80F - 1600 + 1500 + 1300 = 0\\ 4.80F &= 1200 \;\Rightarrow\; F = 250~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

$F$ is positive, so the link is in tension, as assumed. Its force on the bar is $250(-0.8,\,-0.6)=(-200,\,-150)$ N.

Force sums:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= A_x - 200 + 500 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = -300~\text{N},\\ \sum F_y &= A_y - 150 - 800 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = +950~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

The negative $A_x$ means the pin pushes the bar to the left. Then

$$A=\sqrt{300^2+950^2}=996~\text{N},\qquad \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{950}{300}\right)=72.5^\circ\ \text{above the }-x\text{ axis}.$$

Independent check, moments about $C$. The link force drops out. Measured from $C$, point $A$ is at $(-4,\,+3)$ and the $800~\text{N}$ load acts at $(-2,\,+3)$. The $500~\text{N}$ force acts at $C$ itself:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum M_C &= \underbrace{(-4)(950)-(3)(-300)}_{\text{pin at }A} + \underbrace{(-2)(-800)}_{800~\text{N}} + 0_{(500~\text{N at }C)} + 1300\\ &= -3800 + 900 + 1600 + 1300 = 0.\ \checkmark \end{aligned}$$

Common mistakes. (i) Multiplying the couple by a distance. It is already a moment of $1300~\text{N·m}$. (ii) Drawing the link with pin components at both $C$ and $E$: that adds three unneeded unknowns and an unsolvable system. (iii) Dropping the $500~\text{N}$ force from $\sum M_A$. Its line of action is $3.00~\text{m}$ below $A$, and a rightward push there turns the bar counterclockwise, contributing $+1500~\text{N·m}$.

Answer

$$\boxed{F_{CE} = 250~\text{N (tension)}}$$ $$\boxed{A_x = 300~\text{N (to the left)}},\qquad \boxed{A_y = 950~\text{N (upward)}}\quad(A=996~\text{N at }72.5^\circ\text{ above the }-x\text{ axis}).$$

Sanity check. Horizontal balance: $-300-200+500=0$. Vertical balance: $950-150-800=0$. The moment check about $C$ used a different set of unknowns and also closed. Reasonableness: the link pulls the bar down and toward the wall, so $A_y$ must exceed the $800~\text{N}$ load, and $950~\text{N}$ does, by exactly the $150~\text{N}$ vertical part of the link force. The pin pushes left because the $500~\text{N}$ push to the right is larger than the $200~\text{N}$ pull toward the wall from the link. Finally, about $A$ the counterclockwise moments are the couple and the $500~\text{N}$ force ($1300+1500=2800~\text{N·m}$) and the clockwise ones are the $800~\text{N}$ load and the link ($1600+1200=2800~\text{N·m}$), so they balance.

10. 3D equilibrium: a plate on a ball-and-socket and three cables

Level: exam-level. Chapter 4. Target confusion: in 3D there are six equations and a cable force must be written as a magnitude times a unit vector; a moment about a well-chosen axis removes several unknowns at once.

Given / Find

A thin, rigid, rectangular plate $ABCD$ lies horizontally. With origin at $A$ and axes $x$ (along $AB$), $y$ (along $AD$) and $z$ (vertically up), the corners are $A(0,0,0)$, $B(4,0,0)$, $C(4,3,0)$ and $D(0,3,0)$, in meters. The weight of the plate is negligible. The plate is held by a ball-and-socket joint at $A$ and by three cables:

  • cable $BG$ is vertical, from $B$ up to $G(4,0,3)$;
  • cable $DE$ is vertical, from $D$ up to $E(0,3,3)$;
  • cable $CE$ runs from $C$ up to the same anchor $E(0,3,3)$.

A force $\mathbf P=(600\,\mathbf i-900\,\mathbf k)~\text{N}$ acts on the plate at the point $(3,2,0)$ m.

Find: the three cable tensions $T_B$, $T_D$, $T_C$ and the reaction at $A$ in vector form.

Figure (see PDF for drawing)
Oblique view. The plate $ABCD$ is horizontal; $x$ runs to the right, $y$ runs away from the viewer, and $z$ is up (a right-handed set). The ball-and-socket is at $A$. Cables $BG$ and $DE$ are vertical ($3.00~\text{m}$ long). Cable $CE$ goes from $C$ to the same anchor $E$ as cable $DE$: $4.00~\text{m}$ toward the $-x$ direction and $3.00~\text{m}$ up. The force $\mathbf P$ acts at $(3,2,0)$ m with components $600~\text{N}$ in $+x$ and $900~\text{N}$ in $-z$.
Free-body diagram: the plate with the known force $\mathbf P$ and six unknowns. At $A$ the ball-and-socket supplies three components $A_x$, $A_y$, $A_z$ (drawn in their positive senses). Each cable can only pull, so $T_B$, $T_D$, $T_C$ are drawn toward their anchors: straight up at $B$ and $D$, and up and toward the $-x$ direction at $C$ along $CE$.

Strategy

Unknowns and equations. A ball-and-socket gives three force components and each cable gives one tension, so there are six unknowns. In 3D, $\sum\mathbf F=\mathbf 0$ and $\sum\mathbf M=\mathbf 0$ give six scalar equations. Six equations, six unknowns.

Cable direction. Write each cable force as (tension)$\times$(unit vector from the plate toward the anchor):

  • $BG$ and $DE$: unit vector $\mathbf k$;
  • $CE$: from $C(4,3,0)$ to $E(0,3,3)$ the vector is $(-4,0,3)$, length $5$, so the unit vector is $(-0.8,\,0,\,0.6)$.

Moment center and axes. Take moments about $A$ with $\mathbf M_A=\sum \mathbf r\times\mathbf F=\mathbf 0$. The ball-and-socket force passes through $A$, so none of $A_x,A_y,A_z$ appear. Each component of this vector equation is a moment about an axis through $A$. Choosing the axis well removes more unknowns:

  • the $z$-axis through $A$: a vertical force has no moment about a vertical axis, so the two vertical cables drop out and only $T_C$ and $\mathbf P$ remain;
  • the $x$-axis is the line $AB$. It passes through two unknown forces, the ball-and-socket at $A$ and the cable at $B$, so the moments of $A_x,A_y,A_z$ and $T_B$ about it are all zero, leaving only $T_D$ and $T_C$.

The force sums then give the ball-and-socket reaction.

Solution

Position vectors from $A$ (m): $\mathbf r_B=4\mathbf i$, $\mathbf r_C=4\mathbf i+3\mathbf j$, $\mathbf r_D=3\mathbf j$, $\mathbf r_P=3\mathbf i+2\mathbf j$.

Forces on the plate (N): $\mathbf T_B=T_B\mathbf k$, $\mathbf T_D=T_D\mathbf k$, $\mathbf T_C=T_C(-0.8\,\mathbf i+0.6\,\mathbf k)$, $\mathbf P=600\,\mathbf i-900\,\mathbf k$, and $\mathbf A=A_x\mathbf i+A_y\mathbf j+A_z\mathbf k$.

Moment of each force about $A$ (using $\mathbf i\times\mathbf j=\mathbf k$, $\mathbf j\times\mathbf k=\mathbf i$, $\mathbf k\times\mathbf i=\mathbf j$, so $\mathbf i\times\mathbf k=-\mathbf j$):

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf r_B\times\mathbf T_B &= (4\mathbf i)\times(T_B\mathbf k) = -4T_B\,\mathbf j,\\ \mathbf r_D\times\mathbf T_D &= (3\mathbf j)\times(T_D\mathbf k) = 3T_D\,\mathbf i,\\ \mathbf r_C\times\mathbf T_C &= (4\mathbf i+3\mathbf j)\times T_C(-0.8\,\mathbf i+0.6\,\mathbf k) = T_C\,(1.8\,\mathbf i-2.4\,\mathbf j+2.4\,\mathbf k),\\ \mathbf r_P\times\mathbf P &= (3\mathbf i+2\mathbf j)\times(600\,\mathbf i-900\,\mathbf k) = -1800\,\mathbf i+2700\,\mathbf j-1200\,\mathbf k. \end{aligned}$$

Adding them and setting each component of $\sum\mathbf M_A$ to zero:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum M_z:&\quad 2.4\,T_C-1200=0 &&\Rightarrow\ T_C=500~\text{N},\\ \sum M_x:&\quad 3\,T_D+1.8\,T_C-1800=0 &&\Rightarrow\ 3T_D=1800-900\ \Rightarrow\ T_D=300~\text{N},\\ \sum M_y:&\quad -4\,T_B-2.4\,T_C+2700=0 &&\Rightarrow\ 4T_B=2700-1200\ \Rightarrow\ T_B=375~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

The $\sum M_x$ line above is the moment about the axis $AB$ through two unknowns ($A$ and $B$). Notice that $A_x,A_y,A_z$ and $T_B$ never appear in it.

Force sums. With $\mathbf T_C=500(-0.8,\,0,\,0.6)=(-400,\,0,\,300)$ N:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= A_x - 400 + 600 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = -200~\text{N},\\ \sum F_y &= A_y = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 0,\\ \sum F_z &= A_z + 375 + 300 + 300 - 900 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_z = -75~\text{N}. \end{aligned}$$

So $\mathbf A=(-200\,\mathbf i-75\,\mathbf k)$ N, and $|\mathbf A|=\sqrt{200^2+75^2}=214~\text{N}$.

Independent check, moment about the line $BC$ (the axis through $B$ parallel to $y$, which contains the points of application of $T_B$ and $T_C$, so both drop out). Measured from $B$, $A$ is at $(-4,0,0)$, $D$ at $(-4,3,0)$ and the load point at $(-1,2,0)$. The $y$-components are

$$\begin{aligned} \sum M_{BC} &= \underbrace{4A_z}_{\text{ball at }A} + \underbrace{4T_D}_{\text{cable }DE} + \underbrace{(-900)}_{\mathbf P}\\ &= 4(-75) + 4(300) - 900 = -300 + 1200 - 900 = 0.\ \checkmark \end{aligned}$$

This equation used the reaction and a tension found from different equations, so it tests the whole solution.

Common mistakes. (i) Treating cable $CE$ as vertical. It has a $-0.8$ horizontal part, and that part alone supplies the moment about $z$ that fixes $T_C$. (ii) A sign slip in $\mathbf i\times\mathbf k=-\mathbf j$. (iii) Using 2D thinking: with only $\sum F_x$, $\sum F_y$ and one moment, the problem cannot be solved.

Answer

$$\boxed{T_B = 375~\text{N}},\qquad \boxed{T_D = 300~\text{N}},\qquad \boxed{T_C = 500~\text{N}}$$ $$\boxed{\mathbf A = (-200\,\mathbf i + 0\,\mathbf j - 75\,\mathbf k)~\text{N}}\quad(|\mathbf A|=214~\text{N}:\ 200~\text{N toward }-x,\ 75~\text{N downward}).$$

Sanity check. All three tensions are positive, consistent with cables that can only pull. Vertical balance: the cables supply $375+300+0.6(500)=975~\text{N}$ upward against the $900~\text{N}$ downward part of $\mathbf P$, so the ball-and-socket must supply $75~\text{N}$ downward, matching $A_z=-75~\text{N}$. Horizontal balance: $\mathbf P$ pushes $600~\text{N}$ toward $+x$, cable $CE$ pulls $400~\text{N}$ toward $-x$, and the socket supplies the remaining $200~\text{N}$ toward $-x$. No force has a $y$-component, so $A_y=0$ is expected. The moment check about the line $BC$ closed with a different combination of unknowns.
