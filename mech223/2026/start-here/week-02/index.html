1. Why you are reading this

This week we stay in Beer & Johnston Chapter 2: representing forces as planar vectors and combining them. Lecture will move quickly through components and resultants if you already own the vocabulary below.

2. A force is a vector

In 2D, a force has magnitude and direction in a plane. We usually write it with unit vectors i and j (or resolve it into x and y components). Adding forces means adding vectors—not adding magnitudes unless the forces are collinear and the same sense.

3. Resultant of concurrent forces

If several forces meet at a point (a particle model), their resultant is a single force that has the same effect. Graphically you can tip-to-tail or use the parallelogram; analytically you sum components:
∑Fx and ∑Fy, then rebuild magnitude and angle if needed.
