1. Setting up the determinant (Sec. 3.1)

Theme: which numbers go in which row of the determinant.

For the moment of \(\mathbf{F}\) about point \(O\), \(\mathbf{M}_O=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}\). The rows always follow the order of the product:

\[ \mathbf{M}_O=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}= \begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k}\\ r_x & r_y & r_z\\ F_x & F_y & F_z\end{vmatrix} =(r_yF_z-r_zF_y)\,\mathbf{i}-(r_xF_z-r_zF_x)\,\mathbf{j}+(r_xF_y-r_yF_x)\,\mathbf{k} \]
  • Row 1: the unit vectors \(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}\).
  • Row 2: the first vector, \(\mathbf{r}\): from \(O\) to any point on the line of action of \(\mathbf{F}\) (units: m).
  • Row 3: the second vector, \(\mathbf{F}\) (units: N).

Order matters. Swapping rows 2 and 3 flips every sign: \(\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{r}=-(\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F})\). Also watch the minus sign in front of the \(\mathbf{j}\) term.

Worked example

\(\mathbf{r}=(2\,\mathbf{i}-1\,\mathbf{j}+3\,\mathbf{k})\) m and \(\mathbf{F}=(4\,\mathbf{i}+2\,\mathbf{j}-5\,\mathbf{k})\) N.

\[ \begin{aligned} M_x&=r_yF_z-r_zF_y=(-1)(-5)-(3)(2)=5-6=-1\\ M_y&=-(r_xF_z-r_zF_x)=-[(2)(-5)-(3)(4)]=-(-22)=22\\ M_z&=r_xF_y-r_yF_x=(2)(2)-(-1)(4)=4+4=8 \end{aligned} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \mathbf{M}_O=(-1\,\mathbf{i}+22\,\mathbf{j}+8\,\mathbf{k})\ \text{N}{\cdot}\text{m} \]

Self-check: \(\mathbf{r}=(1\,\mathbf{i}+2\,\mathbf{j})\) m, \(\mathbf{F}=10\,\mathbf{k}\) N. Find \(\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}\).

Answer: self-check 1 (cross product)\((20\,\mathbf{i}-10\,\mathbf{j})\) N·m. (\(M_x=(2)(10)-0=20\); \(M_y=-[(1)(10)-0]=-10\); \(M_z=0\).)

2. Moment of a force about a line (Sec. 3.2C)

Theme: finding the moment of a force about a line.

The moment of \(\mathbf{F}\) about an axis \(OL\) is the part of \(\mathbf{M}_O\) that lies along the axis. It is a scalar, found with the mixed triple product:

\[ M_{OL}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot(\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F})= \begin{vmatrix}\lambda_x & \lambda_y & \lambda_z\\ r_x & r_y & r_z\\ F_x & F_y & F_z\end{vmatrix} \]
  • \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\): unit vector along the line (divide by its length first).
  • \(\mathbf{r}\): from any point on the line to any point on the line of action of \(\mathbf{F}\).
  • Sign: positive means the moment points along \(+\boldsymbol{\lambda}\); negative means opposite. The vector form is \(\mathbf{M}_{OL}=M_{OL}\,\boldsymbol{\lambda}\).

Worked example

The axis runs from \(O(0,0,0)\) to \(B(0,3,4)\) m. A force \(\mathbf{F}=(200\,\mathbf{i}-100\,\mathbf{j}+50\,\mathbf{k})\) N acts at \(A(2,0,0)\) m. Find the moment of \(\mathbf{F}\) about line \(OB\).

  1. Unit vector: \(\overrightarrow{OB}=(0,3,4)\) m, length 5 m, so \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(0,\,0.6,\,0.8)\).
  2. Position vector from a point on the line (\(O\)) to any point on the line of action of \(\mathbf{F}\) (here \(A\)): \(\mathbf{r}=(2,0,0)\) m.
  3. Triple product:
    \[ M_{OB}=\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0.6 & 0.8\\ 2 & 0 & 0\\ 200 & -100 & 50\end{vmatrix} =0-0.6\,[(2)(50)-(0)(200)]+0.8\,[(2)(-100)-(0)(200)]=-60-160=-220\ \text{N}{\cdot}\text{m} \]
  4. Vector form: \(\mathbf{M}_{OB}=-220\,\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(-132\,\mathbf{j}-176\,\mathbf{k})\) N·m. The minus sign means the moment points from \(B\) toward \(O\).

Check: \(\mathbf{M}_O=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}=(0\,\mathbf{i}-100\,\mathbf{j}-200\,\mathbf{k})\) N·m, and \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\mathbf{M}_O=0.6(-100)+0.8(-200)=-220\) N·m. Same answer.

3. More practice: resultant moment in 3D (Sec. 3.1 and 3.4)

Theme: more practice with 3D moments, including the resultant moment of forces that do not share a line of action.

Recipe: pick one point \(O\). For each force, write \(\mathbf{r}_i\) (from \(O\) to any point on the line of action of \(\mathbf{F}_i\)) and \(\mathbf{F}_i\) in components, take \(\mathbf{r}_i\times\mathbf{F}_i\), then add the vectors:

\[ \mathbf{R}=\sum\mathbf{F}_i,\qquad \mathbf{M}^R_O=\sum\mathbf{r}_i\times\mathbf{F}_i \]

Worked example

\(\mathbf{F}_1=-300\,\mathbf{k}\) N acts at \(A(2,0,0)\) m. \(\mathbf{F}_2=150\,\mathbf{j}\) N acts at \(C(1,0,2)\) m. Find the resultant force and the resultant moment about \(O\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{r}_A\times\mathbf{F}_1&=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\2&0&0\\0&0&-300\end{vmatrix}=0\,\mathbf{i}-[(2)(-300)-0]\,\mathbf{j}+0\,\mathbf{k}=600\,\mathbf{j}\ \text{N}{\cdot}\text{m}\\ \mathbf{r}_C\times\mathbf{F}_2&=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\1&0&2\\0&150&0\end{vmatrix}=[0-(2)(150)]\,\mathbf{i}-[0-0]\,\mathbf{j}+[(1)(150)-0]\,\mathbf{k}=-300\,\mathbf{i}+150\,\mathbf{k}\ \text{N}{\cdot}\text{m} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \mathbf{R}=(150\,\mathbf{j}-300\,\mathbf{k})\ \text{N},\qquad \mathbf{M}^R_O=(-300\,\mathbf{i}+600\,\mathbf{j}+150\,\mathbf{k})\ \text{N}{\cdot}\text{m},\qquad |\mathbf{M}^R_O|=\sqrt{300^2+600^2+150^2}\approx 687\ \text{N}{\cdot}\text{m} \]

Self-check: Move \(\mathbf{F}_2\) to act at \(D(0,0,2)\) m instead. What is \(\mathbf{r}_D\times\mathbf{F}_2\)?

Answer: self-check 2 (move F2 to D)\(-300\,\mathbf{i}\) N·m. (\(M_x=(0)(0)-(2)(150)=-300\); \(M_y=-[0-0]=0\); \(M_z=(0)(150)-0=0\).)
