Engineering Statics: Chapter 6 Solved Examples

Example 1

A truss consists of three pin-connected members AB, BC, and CA forming a right triangle. Member AC is horizontal and 2\ \mathrm{m} long, with a pin support at A and a roller support at C (the roller can exert only a vertical force). Joint B is 2\ \mathrm{m} directly above A, so member BC makes 45^\circ with the horizontal at C. A horizontal 500\ \mathrm{N} force acts to the right at B. Determine the force in each member and state whether it is in tension or compression. Also find the support reactions at A and C.

Plan. Use the method of joints, starting at B because it has one known force and only two unknown member forces. Every member force is assumed to be tension, meaning it pulls on the joint along the member, away from the joint. A negative result then means compression. Take +x to the right and +y upward.

Joint B. Member BA pulls B straight down. Member BC pulls B toward C, which is to the right and downward at 45^\circ. \begin{aligned} \sum F_x&=0:& 500+F_{BC}\cos45^\circ&=0 &&\Rightarrow F_{BC}=-707.1\ \mathrm{N}\\ \sum F_y&=0:& -F_{BA}-F_{BC}\sin45^\circ&=0 &&\Rightarrow F_{BA}=-(-707.1)(0.7071)=+500\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned} The negative sign for F_{BC} means that member is in compression.

Joint C. Member CB pulls C up and to the left at 45^\circ, member CA pulls C to the left, and the roller reaction C_y acts vertically. \begin{aligned} \sum F_x&=0:& -F_{CA}-F_{BC}\cos45^\circ&=0 &&\Rightarrow F_{CA}=+500\ \mathrm{N}\\ \sum F_y&=0:& C_y+F_{BC}\sin45^\circ&=0 &&\Rightarrow C_y=+500\ \mathrm{N}\ (\text{up}) \end{aligned}

Joint A. Member AB pulls A up and member AC pulls A to the right. Assume A_x points right and A_y points up. \begin{aligned} \sum F_x&=0:& F_{CA}+A_x&=0 &&\Rightarrow A_x=-500\ \mathrm{N}\\ \sum F_y&=0:& F_{BA}+A_y&=0 &&\Rightarrow A_y=-500\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned} The negative signs mean A_x points left and A_y points down.

Check. For the whole truss, taking counterclockwise moments about A as positive, C_y(2)-500(2)=500(2)-1000=0, and \sum F_y=500-500=0.

\boxed{F_{BC}=707.1\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(C)}},\quad \boxed{F_{BA}=500\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(T)}},\quad \boxed{F_{CA}=500\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(T)}} \boxed{A_x=500\ \mathrm{N}\ \leftarrow},\quad \boxed{A_y=500\ \mathrm{N}\ \downarrow},\quad \boxed{C_y=500\ \mathrm{N}\ \uparrow}

Example 2

A pin-connected truss has joints A, B, C, and D along its bottom chord, spaced 4\ \mathrm{m} apart, so AD=12\ \mathrm{m}. Joints G and E are on the top chord, 3\ \mathrm{m} directly above B and C respectively. The members are AB, BC, CD, AG, GE, ED, GB, EC, and GC. The truss has a pin support at A and a roller support at D. A 1200\ \mathrm{N} force acts downward at C, and a 400\ \mathrm{N} force acts horizontally to the right at E. A section line a–a passes through members GE, GC, and BC only. Determine the forces in members GE, GC, and BC, and state whether each is in tension or compression.

Support reactions. The section method needs the external reactions first. For the whole truss, assume A_x points right and A_y and D_y point up. Counterclockwise moments are positive. The 400\ \mathrm{N} force acts 3\ \mathrm{m} above A, and the 1200\ \mathrm{N} force acts 8\ \mathrm{m} to the right of A. \begin{aligned} \sum F_x&=0:& 400+A_x&=0 &&\Rightarrow A_x=-400\ \mathrm{N}\ (\text{left})\\ \sum M_A&=0:& -1200(8)-400(3)+D_y(12)&=0 &&\Rightarrow D_y=900\ \mathrm{N}\\ \sum F_y&=0:& A_y-1200+900&=0 &&\Rightarrow A_y=300\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned}

Free-body diagram. Cut along a–a and keep the left portion, since all of its external forces are known: A_x=400\ \mathrm{N} to the left and A_y=300\ \mathrm{N} up. Assume each cut member is in tension, so each force pulls away from the left portion. F_{GE} and F_{BC} act to the right. F_{GC} acts along GC, which has a 3–4–5 slope, down and to the right, so its components are \tfrac45F_{GC} and \tfrac35F_{GC}.

Equilibrium. Moments about G eliminate F_{GE} and F_{GC}, because both lines of action pass through G. Here A_y has a 4\ \mathrm{m} arm and tends to turn the portion clockwise, A_x has a 3\ \mathrm{m} arm and also tends to turn it clockwise, and F_{BC} lies 3\ \mathrm{m} below G and tends to turn it counterclockwise. \begin{aligned} \sum M_G&=0:& -300(4)-400(3)+F_{BC}(3)&=0 &&\Rightarrow F_{BC}=+800\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned} Moments about C eliminate F_{GC} and F_{BC}, because both pass through C. Then A_y has an 8\ \mathrm{m} arm (clockwise), A_x has zero arm, and F_{GE} lies 3\ \mathrm{m} above C and acts to the right (clockwise). \begin{aligned} \sum M_C&=0:& -300(8)-F_{GE}(3)&=0 &&\Rightarrow F_{GE}=-800\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned} Since F_{GE} and F_{BC} have no vertical components, a vertical force sum gives F_{GC} directly. \begin{aligned} \sum F_y&=0:& 300-\tfrac35F_{GC}&=0 &&\Rightarrow F_{GC}=+500\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned} Check. \sum F_x=-400+F_{BC}+F_{GE}+\tfrac45F_{GC}=-400+800-800+400=0. A positive result means tension and a negative result means compression.

\boxed{F_{BC}=800\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(T)}},\quad \boxed{F_{GE}=800\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(C)}},\quad \boxed{F_{GC}=500\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(T)}}

Example 3

A pin-connected roof truss has joints A, B, C, D, and E along its horizontal bottom chord, spaced 4\ \mathrm{m} apart, so AE=16\ \mathrm{m}. The truss has a pin support at A and a roller support at E. Top joint G is 6\ \mathrm{m} above C, and top joint F is 4\ \mathrm{m} above D. Diagonal CF therefore makes 45^\circ with the horizontal. A 5\ \mathrm{kN} load acts downward at C and a 3\ \mathrm{kN} load acts downward at D. A section line a–a passes through members GF, CF, and CD only. Determine the force in member CF and state whether it is in tension or compression.

Reactions. With A at the origin, x to the right, and counterclockwise moments positive: \begin{aligned} \sum M_A&=0:& -5(8)-3(12)+E_y(16)&=0 &&\Rightarrow E_y=4.75\ \mathrm{kN}\\ \sum F_y&=0:& A_y-5-3+4.75&=0 &&\Rightarrow A_y=3.25\ \mathrm{kN} \end{aligned}

Free-body diagram. Cut along a–a and keep the right portion, which contains D, E, and F. It carries the 3\ \mathrm{kN} load at D and the 4.75\ \mathrm{kN} reaction at E. The three unknowns are F_{GF}, F_{CF}, and F_{CD}. Moments about a suitable point O will remove two of them.

Choosing O. Member GF drops 2\ \mathrm{m} over each 4\ \mathrm{m} of horizontal run. To reach the level of the bottom chord from F, it must drop 4\ \mathrm{m} more, which takes 8\ \mathrm{m} of horizontal run. Hence line GF meets the bottom-chord line at O, 8\ \mathrm{m} to the right of D and 4\ \mathrm{m} beyond E. Lines GF and CD both pass through O, so only F_{CF} and the loads appear in \sum M_O=0.

Assume CF is in compression, so on the right portion it pushes up and to the right at 45^\circ. Slide F_{CF} along its line to C and resolve it there. The horizontal component lies along the bottom-chord line through O and has zero moment. The vertical component F_{CF}\sin45^\circ has a 12\ \mathrm{m} arm to O and turns the portion clockwise. The 3\ \mathrm{kN} load (8\ \mathrm{m} arm) turns it counterclockwise, and the 4.75\ \mathrm{kN} reaction (4\ \mathrm{m} arm) turns it clockwise. \begin{aligned} \sum M_O&=0:& -F_{CF}\sin45^\circ(12)+3(8)-4.75(4)&=0\\ F_{CF}&=\frac{24-19}{12\sin45^\circ}=\frac{5}{8.485}=0.589\ \mathrm{kN} \end{aligned} The positive result confirms that the assumed compression is correct.

Check. Moments about F eliminate F_{GF} and F_{CF}, which both pass through F, and the vertical 3\ \mathrm{kN} load, which also passes through F. This leaves 4.75(4)-F_{CD}(4)=0, so F_{CD}=4.75\ \mathrm{kN} (T). The force balances \sum F_x=0 and \sum F_y=0 on the right portion then give F_{GF}=4.845\ \mathrm{kN} (C), and both sums reduce to zero.

\boxed{F_{CF}=0.589\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(C)}}

Example 4

A load of 600\ \mathrm{N} hangs from the hook of frictionless pulley A. One continuous weightless cable runs over pulleys A, B, and C, and its free end is pulled downward by a force P. Pulley C hangs from a fixed ceiling support. Pulley B is joined to the pin of C by a short connecting cable, which is a two-force member carrying a tension T. The cable arrangement puts three vertical cable segments on A pulling upward, two vertical segments on B pulling downward, and two vertical segments on C pulling downward (one of them is the free end). The weights of the pulleys and cable are neglected. Determine the cable tension P needed to support the 600\ \mathrm{N} load, the connecting-cable tension T, and the ceiling support force R on pulley C.

Free-body diagrams. Draw each pulley separately, including its pin and the piece of cable touching it. The cable is continuous over frictionless pulleys, so its tension is the same value P everywhere. The connecting cable between B and C has an unknown tension T. It pulls B upward and pulls C downward, an equal and opposite pair. The ceiling exerts an upward force R on C. Take upward as positive for \sum F_y.

Pulley A. Three cable segments pull upward, and the load pulls downward. \begin{aligned} \sum F_y&=0:& 3P-600&=0 &&\Rightarrow P=200\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned}

Pulley B. The connecting cable pulls upward with T, and two cable segments pull downward with P each. \begin{aligned} \sum F_y&=0:& T-2P&=0 &&\Rightarrow T=2(200)=400\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned}

Pulley C. The ceiling force R acts upward. Two cable segments pull downward with P each, and the connecting cable pulls downward with T. \begin{aligned} \sum F_y&=0:& R-2P-T&=0 &&\Rightarrow R=2(200)+400=800\ \mathrm{N} \end{aligned}

Check. Take all three pulleys together as one system. The external forces are the ceiling force R upward, the 600\ \mathrm{N} load downward, and the free-end force P downward. This gives R=600+P=800\ \mathrm{N}, which agrees.

\boxed{P=200\ \mathrm{N}},\quad \boxed{T=400\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(tension)}},\quad \boxed{R=800\ \mathrm{N}\ \uparrow}
