Conventions. Right and up are positive. Counterclockwise (CCW) moments are positive. A member force is tension (T) if positive (it pulls on the joints at its ends) and compression (C) if negative (it pushes on them). In joint equations, a tensile force acts on a joint toward the other end of the member. Numbers are rounded to three significant figures.

Key status. Each example says whether a typed or published key exists. Where none exists, it is marked independently solved, and the result was also confirmed by solving the full set of joint (or member) equations numerically.

1. Truss on two wall pins: zero vertical reaction at C

Source: MECH 223 F2022, Homework 9, Q1. Method of joints. Independently solved; no typed key on file.

Given / Find

Determine the force in each member and state T or C. Hint: the vertical component of force at \(C\) must be zero. Why?

Joint \(A\) carries \(6~\text{kN}\) down and joint \(E\) carries \(8~\text{kN}\) down. The truss is pinned to a wall at \(C\) (top) and \(D\) (bottom), \(2~\text{m}\) apart vertically. \(AE=1.5~\text{m}\), \(ED=2~\text{m}\), \(B\) lies directly above \(E\), and \(BC\) is horizontal. Members: \(AB, AE, ED, BE, BC, BD\).

Strategy

Only the horizontal member \(BC\) reaches \(C\), and a two-force member pushes or pulls only along its axis, so \(C_y=0\). Moments about \(D\) give \(C_x\); then walk joints \(A\), \(E\), \(B\), and keep \(D\) as a check.

Solution

Coordinates (m), origin at \(A\): \(A(0,0)\), \(E(1.5,0)\), \(D(3.5,0)\), \(B(1.5,2)\), \(C(3.5,2)\). Member \(AB\) has length \(2.5~\text{m}\), direction cosines \((0.6,\,0.8)\). Member \(BD\) is at \(45^\circ\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum M_D &= 6(3.5)+8(2)-C_x(2)=0 \Rightarrow C_x=18.5~\text{kN (right)},\\ \sum F_x &= C_x+D_x=0 \Rightarrow D_x=18.5~\text{kN (left)},\\ \sum F_y &= D_y-6-8=0 \Rightarrow D_y=14.0~\text{kN (up)}. \end{aligned} \]

Joint \(A\).

\[ 0.8F_{AB}-6=0\Rightarrow F_{AB}=7.50~\text{kN (T)},\qquad 0.6(7.50)+F_{AE}=0\Rightarrow F_{AE}=-4.50~\text{kN (C)}. \]

Joint \(E\). \(AE\) pulls \(E\) toward \(A\), so it enters as \(-F_{AE}\).

\[ -F_{AE}+F_{ED}=0\Rightarrow F_{ED}=-4.50~\text{kN (C)},\qquad F_{BE}-8=0\Rightarrow F_{BE}=8.00~\text{kN (T)}. \]

Joint \(B\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum F_y &= -0.8(7.50)-8.00-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}F_{BD}=0 \Rightarrow F_{BD}=-14\sqrt2=-19.8~\text{kN (C)},\\ \sum F_x &= -0.6(7.50)+F_{BC}+\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(-19.8)=0 \Rightarrow F_{BC}=4.5+14=18.5~\text{kN (T)}. \end{aligned} \]

Check at joint \(D\). \(ED\) (C) pushes \(D\) right with \(4.50\); \(BD\) (C) pushes along \(B\to D\) with \((+14,-14)\); the pin gives \((-18.5,+14)\). Sum in \(x\): \(4.5+14-18.5=0\). Sum in \(y\): \(-14+14=0\). Count: \(m+r=6+4=10=2n\) with \(n=5\), determinate.

2. Bridge truss: members GB and GF

Source: MECH 223 F2022, Homework 9, Q2. Method of joints. Independently solved; no typed key on file.

Given / Find

Find the force in members \(GB\) and \(GF\) and state T or C. Pin at \(A\), roller at \(D\), span \(AD=10+4+4+10=28~\text{ft}\), truss height \(10~\text{ft}\). Bottom joints \(A, B, C, D\) at \(x=0,10,18,28\); top joints \(G, F, E\) at \(x=10,14,18\). \(600~\text{lb}\) down at \(B\) and \(800~\text{lb}\) down at \(C\).

Strategy

Get the reactions. Joint \(A\) has two unknown members (\(AG\), \(AB\)). Joint \(G\) then has three members (\(GA\), \(GB\), \(GF\)) with \(GA\) known, so two equations give \(GB\) and \(GF\).

Solution

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum M_A &= D_y(28)-600(10)-800(18)=0 \Rightarrow D_y=\tfrac{20400}{28}=728.6~\text{lb},\\ \sum F_y &= 0 \Rightarrow A_y=1400-728.6=671.4~\text{lb},\qquad A_x=0. \end{aligned} \]

Joint \(A\) (\(AG\) rises at \(45^\circ\)).

\[ 671.4+\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}F_{AG}=0\Rightarrow F_{AG}=-949.5~\text{lb (C)},\qquad F_{AB}+\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(-949.5)=0\Rightarrow F_{AB}=671.4~\text{lb (T)}. \]

Joint \(G\). \(GA\) in compression pushes \(G\) up and right with \((+671.4,+671.4)\). \(GB\) hangs straight down; \(GF\) runs right along the top chord.

\[ 671.4-F_{GB}=0\Rightarrow F_{GB}=671.4~\text{lb (T)},\qquad 671.4+F_{GF}=0\Rightarrow F_{GF}=-671.4~\text{lb (C)}. \]

Sense check. The top chord of a simply supported truss under downward loads is in compression, and a vertical member hanging a loaded lower joint from the top is in tension. A full solve of all 14 joint equations (11 members, 3 reactions) agrees.

Sign flag. An earlier draft of this set listed \(GB\) as compression and \(GF\) as tension. The magnitudes match, but the labels are reversed relative to the derivation above: the vertical component of the compressive force in \(GA\) must be balanced by a downward pull from \(GB\), so \(GB\) is in tension. This page uses the derived signs.

\[ \boxed{F_{GB}=671~\text{lb (T)},\quad F_{GF}=671~\text{lb (C)}}\qquad (A_x=0,\ A_y=671~\text{lb},\ D_y=729~\text{lb}) \]

3. Bridge deck truss: members JI and EI

Source: MECH 223 F2022, Homework 9, Q3. Method of sections, then joint \(E\). Independently solved; no typed key on file.

Given / Find

Find the force in members \(EI\) and \(JI\) and state T or C. Six panels of \(9~\text{ft}\) (total \(54~\text{ft}\)), height \(12~\text{ft}\), pin at \(A\), roller at \(G\). Top joints \(A\) to \(G\) at \(x=0,9,\dots,54\); bottom joints \(L,K,J,I,H\) at \(x=9,18,27,36,45\). Loads: \(4000~\text{lb}\) down at \(B\), \(8000~\text{lb}\) at \(C\), \(5000~\text{lb}\) at \(F\).

Strategy

A section between \(D\) and \(E\) cuts exactly three members (\(DE\), \(EJ\), \(JI\)). A moment equation about \(E\) isolates \(JI\). \(EI\) is not cut, so finish with joint \(E\), where \(DE\) and \(EF\) are both horizontal and one vertical equation gives \(EI\).

Solution

Diagonals have run 9 and rise 12, so they are 3-4-5 members with direction cosines \((0.6,\,0.8)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum M_A &= G_y(54)-4000(9)-8000(18)-5000(45)=0 \Rightarrow G_y=\tfrac{405\,000}{54}=7500~\text{lb},\\ A_y &= 17\,000-7500=9500~\text{lb},\qquad A_x=0. \end{aligned} \]

Section, right free body (\(E,F,G,H,I\); assume all cut members in tension). \(DE\) and \(EJ\) pass through \(E\):

\[ \sum M_E=G_y(18)-5000(9)-T_{JI}(12)=0\Rightarrow 135\,000-45\,000=12\,T_{JI}\Rightarrow F_{JI}=7500~\text{lb (T)}. \]

Vertical equation on the same free body (\(EJ\) pulls toward \(J\), vertical component \(-0.8F_{EJ}\)):

\[ 7500-5000-0.8F_{EJ}=0\Rightarrow F_{EJ}=3125~\text{lb (T)}. \]

Joint \(E\).

\[ \sum F_y=-F_{EI}-0.8(3125)=0\Rightarrow F_{EI}=-2500~\text{lb (C)}. \]

Check. \(\sum F_x=0\) on the free body gives \(F_{DE}=-9375~\text{lb}\) (C): top chord in compression, bottom chord in tension. Count: \(m+r=21+3=24=2n\) with \(n=12\). A full solve of the 24 joint equations gives \(F_{JI}=+7500\) and \(F_{EI}=-2500~\text{lb}\).

\[ \boxed{F_{JI}=7500~\text{lb (T)},\quad F_{EI}=2500~\text{lb (C)}}\qquad (A_x=0,\ A_y=9500~\text{lb},\ G_y=7500~\text{lb}) \]

4. Truss support reactions, two methods

Source: course-pages published worked example, “Truss Support Reactions: Two Equivalent Approaches” (mech223/2026/truss-reactions-worked-example). The numbers below were compared with that page; it is the key.

Given / Find

Find the reactions. \(A\) is a pin (\(A_x\), \(A_y\)); \(B\) is a rocker (\(B_y\) only). Origin at \(A\): \(B\) at \(x=6~\text{m}\). A \(6~\text{kN}\) load acts down at \(x=2\), an \(8~\text{kN}\) load acts down at \(x=4\), and a \(5~\text{kN}\) load acts horizontally to the right at the top joint, \(2~\text{m}\) above \(A\). The member layout does not affect the reactions.

Method 1: equations of equilibrium

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= A_x+5=0 \Rightarrow A_x=-5~\text{kN (5 kN left)},\\ \sum M_A &= B_y(6)-6(2)-8(4)-5(2)=0 \Rightarrow B_y=\tfrac{54}{6}=9~\text{kN},\\ \sum F_y &= A_y+9-6-8=0 \Rightarrow A_y=5~\text{kN}. \end{aligned} \]

Method 2: equivalent force-couple system at \(A\)

Replace the three loads by one force and one couple at \(A\):

\[ \mathbf F_R=(5\,\mathbf i-14\,\mathbf j)~\text{kN},\qquad M_{R_A}=-6(2)-8(4)-5(2)=-54~\text{kN·m}. \]

The supports must cancel this exactly. The pin cancels the horizontal part, so \(A_x=-5\); \(A_y+B_y=14\); only \(B_y\), acting \(6~\text{m}\) from \(A\), cancels the couple: \(6B_y=54\), so \(B_y=9\) and \(A_y=5~\text{kN}\).

Why they agree. The two methods are the same three equations: horizontal \(A_x+5=0\), vertical \(A_y+B_y-14=0\), rotation \(6B_y-54=0\). The \(5~\text{kN}\) load and \(A_x\) are equal, opposite, parallel and \(2~\text{m}\) apart, so they form a \(10~\text{kN·m}\) clockwise couple that the vertical reactions must counter.

Check. Moments about \(B\): \(-5(6)+6(4)+8(2)-5(2)=-30+24+16-10=0\).

\[ \boxed{A_x=5~\text{kN}\ (\leftarrow),\quad A_y=5~\text{kN}\ (\uparrow),\quad B_y=9~\text{kN}\ (\uparrow)} \]

5. Frame with a pulley: force at C

Source: MECH 223 F2022, Homework 10, Q1, compared with the instructor key 22_FA_MECH223_HW10_Sol. Frames, multi-member equilibrium.

Given / Find

Find the horizontal and vertical components of the force at \(C\) that member \(ABC\) exerts on member \(CEF\). The frame is pinned at \(A\) and on a roller at \(F\). Pins \(B\), \(C\), \(E\) join members \(ABC\), \(BED\), \(CEF\). A pulley of radius \(1~\text{ft}\) sits on pin \(D\) at the end of \(BED\). A cable runs from a ring on \(CEF\), horizontally to the top of the pulley, around it, and down, with \(300~\text{lb}\) tension on the hanging side.

Coordinates (ft), origin at \(A\): \(A(0,0)\), \(B(3,4)\), \(C(6,8)\), \(E(6,4)\), \(F(6,0)\), \(D(9,4)\); the ring is on \(CEF\) at \((6,5)\).

Strategy

Three members and three pins give nine equations in nine unknowns (two components at each of \(A,B,C,E\), plus \(F_y\)). The cable pulls the pulley with \(300~\text{lb}\) along each segment leaving it, so pin \(D\) carries \(300~\text{lb}\) left and \(300~\text{lb}\) down. The same cable also pulls the ring on \(CEF\) with \(300~\text{lb}\) to the right. That load is easy to miss and is required for \(E_x\).

Solution

Member \(BED\) (loads at \(D\): \(300\) left, \(300\) down).

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum M_B &= E_y(3)-300(6)=0 \Rightarrow E_y=600~\text{lb},\\ \sum F_y &= B_y+600-300=0 \Rightarrow B_y=-300~\text{lb (300 lb down at B)},\\ \sum F_x &= B_x+E_x-300=0. \quad (1) \end{aligned} \]

Member \(CEF\) (at \(E\): \(-E_x\), \(-600\) from \(BED\); \(300~\text{lb}\) right at the ring). Moments about \(C\) remove \(C_x\), \(C_y\) and the vertical forces on \(x=6\):

\[ \sum M_C=300(3)-E_x(4)=0\Rightarrow E_x=225~\text{lb},\qquad \sum F_x=C_x-225+300=0\Rightarrow C_x=-75~\text{lb}. \]

From (1): \(B_x=300-225=75~\text{lb}\).

Member \(ABC\) (at \(B\): \((-75,+300)\); at \(C\): \((+75,-C_y)\)). Moments about \(A\):

\[ 3(300)-4(-75)+6(-C_y)-8(75)=0\Rightarrow 900+300-6C_y-600=0\Rightarrow C_y=100~\text{lb}. \]

Independent check. Whole frame, moments about \(A\), cable counted once as \(300~\text{lb}\) down at \(x=10\): \(6F_y=3000\), \(F_y=500~\text{lb}\). Member \(CEF\) vertical: \(C_y-600+F_y=0\Rightarrow C_y=100\). Member \(ABC\): \(A_y=-200~\text{lb}\), \(A_x=0\); and \(A_y+F_y=300\), equal to the hanging load.

Match to the key. The typed instructor key gives \(E_y=600\), \(B_y=300\), \(E_x=225\), \(B_x=75\), \(C_x=75\), \(C_y=100~\text{lb}\). All agree.

\[ \boxed{\text{ABC on CEF at C:}\quad C_x=75~\text{lb}\ (\leftarrow),\quad C_y=100~\text{lb}\ (\uparrow)} \]

6. Cantilever truss from a wall: all member forces and determinacy

Source: MECH 223 F2024 exam, Problem 1 (regular paper, not a makeup), file Exam3-2024.pdf. Independently solved; no key on file.

Given / Find

(a) Find the force in each member and state T or C. (b) Decide whether the truss is statically determinate (\(m+r=2n\)) or indeterminate (\(m+r>2n\)).

Pins at \(A\) (top) and \(E\) (bottom) on a wall, \(4~\text{m}\) apart. Top chord \(A\)–\(B\)–\(C\), \(3~\text{m}+3~\text{m}\). Loads: \(3~\text{kN}\) down at \(B\), \(2~\text{kN}\) down at \(C\). Members: \(AB, BC, BD, AD, ED, DC\).

Assumed coordinates. The position of \(D\) is not dimensioned on the exam. An earlier brief suggested \(D(3,0)\), directly above \(E\). That would make \(ED\) horizontal, which does not match the printed figure. The figure shows \(D\) on the straight line from \(E\) to \(C\), directly below \(B\) at mid-height, so this page assumes (m) \(E(0,0)\), \(A(0,4)\), \(B(3,4)\), \(C(6,4)\), \(D(3,2)\). All results below are for that geometry.

Strategy

Joint \(C\) has only two members, so start at the loaded tip with no reactions needed. Work \(C\), \(B\), \(D\); reactions follow from joints \(E\) and \(A\). Let \(s=\sqrt{13}=3.606\); diagonals have direction \((3,2)/s\) in magnitude.

Solution

Joint \(C\) (\(BC\) to the left; \(DC\) toward \(D\), direction \((-3,-2)/s\)).

\[ -2-\tfrac{2}{s}F_{DC}=0\Rightarrow F_{DC}=-\sqrt{13}=-3.61~\text{kN (C)},\qquad -F_{BC}-\tfrac{3}{s}F_{DC}=0\Rightarrow F_{BC}=3.00~\text{kN (T)}. \]

Joint \(B\) (\(BD\) straight down).

\[ -F_{AB}+3.00=0\Rightarrow F_{AB}=3.00~\text{kN (T)},\qquad -3-F_{BD}=0\Rightarrow F_{BD}=-3.00~\text{kN (C)}. \]

Joint \(D\) (\(BD\) up, \(AD\) along \((-3,2)/s\), \(DC\) along \((3,2)/s\), \(DE\) along \((-3,-2)/s\)).

\[ F_{AD}+F_{DE}=-\sqrt{13},\qquad F_{AD}-F_{DE}=\tfrac52\sqrt{13} \;\Rightarrow\; F_{AD}=\tfrac34\sqrt{13}=2.70~\text{kN (T)},\quad F_{DE}=-\tfrac74\sqrt{13}=-6.31~\text{kN (C)}. \]

Reactions. \(ED\) in compression pushes \(E\) with \((-5.25,-3.50)\), so \(E_x=5.25~\text{kN}\) (right), \(E_y=3.50~\text{kN}\) (up). At \(A\), \(AB\) (T) pulls with \((+3.00,0)\) and \(AD\) (T) with \((+2.25,-1.50)\), so \(A_x=5.25~\text{kN}\) (left), \(A_y=1.50~\text{kN}\) (up).

Part (b): determinacy

\(m=6\) members, \(r=4\) reaction components (two pins), \(n=5\) joints:

\[ m+r=10=2n=10\;\Rightarrow\;\text{statically determinate}. \]

The ten joint equations were independent (no leftover equations or unknowns), so the truss is also stable.

Check. Moments about \(A\): \(E_x\) at \(4~\text{m}\) below \(A\) gives \(+21~\text{kN·m}\); the loads give \(-3(3)-2(6)=-21\). Sum \(=0\). Vertical: \(1.50+3.50=5.00=3+2\). A full solve of the ten joint equations agrees.
