Truss Support Reactions: Two Equivalent Approaches

MECH 223 Engineering Statics · Worked example

Problem

Determine the reactions at the supports. A is a pin (reactions \(A_x, A_y\)); B is a rocker (vertical reaction \(B_y\) only).

Coordinates (origin at A): 6 kN at \(x=2\) m, 8 kN at \(x=4\) m, B at \(x=6\) m. The 5 kN load acts horizontally at the top joint, \(y=2\) m above A.

Method 1: Equations of equilibrium

Sign convention: right and up positive; counterclockwise (CCW) moments positive.

$$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x + 5 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = -5\ \text{kN}\ (\text{acts to the left})$$ $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad B_y(6) - 6(2) - 8(4) - 5(2) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; B_y = \frac{54}{6} = 9\ \text{kN}$$ $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + 9 - 6 - 8 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 5\ \text{kN}$$
The 5 kN force points right along a line 2 m above A, so it tends to rotate the truss clockwise about A: its moment is \(-(5)(2) = -10\) kN·m. Using the cross product, \(M = x F_y - y F_x = 0 - (2)(5) = -10\) kN·m.

Method 2: Transfer all loads to A (equivalent force–couple system)

Replace the three applied loads by one resultant force and one couple moment at A.

$$\mathbf{F}_R = \sum \mathbf{F} = (5\,\mathbf{i} - 6\,\mathbf{j} - 8\,\mathbf{j}) = (5\,\mathbf{i} - 14\,\mathbf{j})\ \text{kN}$$ $$M_{R_A} = \sum M_A = -6(2) - 8(4) - 5(2) = -54\ \text{kN·m}\ (54\ \text{kN·m clockwise})$$

For equilibrium, the support reactions must exactly cancel this force–couple system:

  • The pin at A absorbs the force: \(A_x = -5\) kN and \(A_y + B_y = 14\) kN.
  • Only \(B_y\), acting 6 m from A, can cancel the couple: \(6B_y = 54 \Rightarrow B_y = 9\) kN, so \(A_y = 5\) kN.

Comparing the two methods

Method 1: ΣF, ΣM = 0Method 2: Resultant at A
Horizontal\(A_x + 5 = 0\)\(A_x\) cancels \(F_{R,x} = 5\)
Vertical\(A_y + B_y - 14 = 0\)\(A_y + B_y\) cancels \(F_{R,y} = -14\)
Rotation\(6B_y - 54 = 0\)\(6B_y\) cancels \(M_{R_A} = -54\)

They are the same three equations, organized differently. \(\sum F_x\) and \(\sum F_y\) are the components of \(\mathbf{F}_R\); \(\sum M_A\) is \(M_{R_A}\) plus the moment of \(B_y\). Method 2 separates two questions: what do the loads do at A? and how do the supports cancel it?

Student question: \(A_x\) and the 5 kN act on different lines. How is that accounted for?

Short answer: \(\sum F_x = 0\) only checks that the horizontal forces add up to zero, wherever they act. The fact that they are on different lines of action is captured by the moment equation.

\(A_x\) (5 kN left, along the bottom chord) and the 5 kN load (right, 2 m higher) are equal, opposite, and parallel but not collinear. Together they form a couple:

$$M_{\text{couple}} = F\,d = 5 \times 2 = 10\ \text{kN·m (clockwise)}$$

That couple is exactly the \(-5(2)\) term in \(\sum M_A\). Since the horizontal forces cannot cancel their own turning effect, the vertical forces do it:

Case\(A_y\)\(B_y\)
Without the 5 kN load6.67 kN7.33 kN
With the 5 kN load5.00 kN9.00 kN
Change−1.67 kN+1.67 kN

The added pair \(\pm 1.67\) kN is 6 m apart, giving \(1.67 \times 6 = 10\) kN·m CCW, which cancels the 10 kN·m clockwise couple.

Link to Method 2: sliding the 5 kN force down to A's line of action is only allowed if you add the couple it leaves behind, \(5 \times 2 = 10\) kN·m clockwise. That is the same 10 kN·m inside \(M_{R_A} = -54\) kN·m.

Takeaway: Equal and opposite forces that are not collinear still form a couple. Balancing forces is not enough; the moment equation catches the turning effect.

Answer and check

\(A_x = 5\) kN \(\leftarrow\), \(\quad A_y = 5\) kN \(\uparrow\), \(\quad B_y = 9\) kN \(\uparrow\)

Check with moments about B (CCW +):

$$\sum M_B = -A_y(6) + 6(4) + 8(2) - 5(2) = -30 + 24 + 16 - 10 = 0 \quad \checkmark$$
