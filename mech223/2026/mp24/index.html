1. First moment of area

Picture a thin flat plate of uniform thickness. The centroid is the point where you could balance the plate on a pin—the average position of the material in the plane. To find that point for an irregular or composite shape, engineers use the first moment of area (sometimes called the statical moment).

About the coordinate axes through the origin:

\[ Q_x = \int y\,dA = \bar{y}\,A \qquad Q_y = \int x\,dA = \bar{x}\,A \]

\(Q_x\) gathers how area is distributed in the \(y\) direction (each strip \(dA\) at height \(y\) contributes \(y\,dA\)). \(Q_y\) does the same for \(x\). Units are length cubed—for example mm\(^3\) or in\(^3\). Sign follows the coordinate: area on the positive side of an axis contributes positive moment about that axis.

Divide by total area to locate the centroid:

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{Q_y}{A} \qquad \bar{y} = \frac{Q_x}{A} \]

If you take moments about an axis that passes through the centroid, the first moment of the whole area about that axis is zero. That is exactly the statement “the centroid is the point where weighted \(y\) (or \(x\)) balances to zero.” Keep that zero property in mind—it is how you will check answers and how shear \(Q\) is defined later.

Composite areas

  1. Split the shape into simple parts (rectangles, triangles, circles, …) and holes as separate rows.
  2. Tabulate \(A_i\), \(\bar{x}_i\), \(\bar{y}_i\), then \(\bar{x}_i A_i\) and \(\bar{y}_i A_i\).
  3. Sum: \(A = \sum A_i\), \(Q_y = \sum \bar{x}_i A_i\), \(Q_x = \sum \bar{y}_i A_i\); then \(\bar{x} = Q_y/A\), \(\bar{y} = Q_x/A\).
  4. Cutouts enter as negative area with the centroid of the removed piece.
  5. Exploit symmetry when a shape is mirrored about an axis—the centroid lies on that axis.

Later in the course, area moments of inertia use the same \(\int (\text{distance})^2\,dA\) idea, and the parallel-axis theorem shifts \(I\) about the centroid. In Mechanics of Materials, the neutral axis of a beam passes through the centroid of the cross section, and transverse shear stress uses \(Q = \int y\,dA\) for the area on one side of a cut about the neutral axis: \(\tau = VQ/(It)\). Same “moment of distribution,” different question.

3. \(Q\) about the centroidal axis — and a T-section preview

For any plane area, the first moment about an axis through the centroid vanishes:

\[ Q_{x,\,\text{centroidal}} = \int (y - \bar{y})\,dA = 0 \qquad Q_{y,\,\text{centroidal}} = \int (x - \bar{x})\,dA = 0 \]

In shear, you will use \(Q\) for only the area on one side of a cut, measured about the neutral axis (through the centroid). That partial area has a nonzero first moment even though the full section’s moment about the NA is zero.

Take the horizontal cut at the NA. Above \(y = 5.50\) in: a \(0.5\) in slice of web (\(A = 1\) in\(^2\), centroid at \(5.75\) in) plus the full flange (\(A = 20\) in\(^2\), centroid at \(7\) in).

\[ Q_{\text{above}} = (5.75 - 5.50)(1) + (7 - 5.50)(20) = 0.25 + 30 = 30.25\ \text{in}^3 \]

Below the NA: web from \(y = 0\) to \(5.50\) in, \(A = 11\) in\(^2\), centroid at \(2.75\) in.

\[ Q_{\text{below}} = (5.50 - 2.75)(11) = 30.25\ \text{in}^3 \]

The two magnitudes match—required because the full section’s first moment about the NA is zero. In \(\tau = VQ/(It)\), it is \(Q\) for the flange side (or an equivalent slice) that sets how shear stress varies over the depth.

Self-check: If the flange were only \(8\) in wide but the web stayed \(2 \times 6\) in, would \(\bar{y}\) move up or down?

Answer

Less flange area high on the section lowers the centroid; \(\bar{y}\) moves down from 5.50 in.

Definitions worth keeping straight

  • First moment \(Q_x\), \(Q_y\). \(\int y\,dA\) and \(\int x\,dA\); link to \(\bar{x}\), \(\bar{y}\) through division by \(A\).
  • Centroid. \(\bar{x} = Q_y/A\), \(\bar{y} = Q_x/A\); first moment about a centroidal axis is zero.
  • Composite part. Sum \(\bar{x}_i A_i\) and \(\bar{y}_i A_i\); holes as negative \(A_i\).
  • Shear \(Q\) (preview). First moment of the area on one side of a cut about the neutral axis. The whole section has zero first moment about that axis; the partial area does not.
